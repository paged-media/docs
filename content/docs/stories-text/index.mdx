Text in IDML lives in **stories**. A story is a stream of text broken into ranges:
runs of text that share a paragraph style, and within those, runs that share
character formatting. Frames on pages then display a story; a long story can thread
through several frames, and the text that doesn't fit is overset.

The pages below build up from the smallest story to the full element model, then
cover the ways a story spans frames and carries computed text:

- 🟢 **[Your first story](/docs/stories-text/your-first-story)** — tutorial: the smallest story that renders a line.
- 🔴 **[Story structure](/docs/stories-text/story-structure)** — reference: the `Story` → `ParagraphStyleRange` → `CharacterStyleRange` → `Content` model.
- 🔴 **[Threading and overset](/docs/stories-text/threading-and-overset)** — explanation: how one story flows across frames, and what happens to text that doesn't fit.
- 🟡 **[Text variables](/docs/stories-text/text-variables)** — reference: how computed text and page-number markers become characters.
- 🟡 **[Extract all text](/docs/stories-text/extract-all-text)** — how-to: walk every story and concatenate its content.
- 🔴 **[Why ranges, not spans](/docs/stories-text/why-ranges-not-spans)** — explanation: why IDML nests ranges instead of wrapping spans.
