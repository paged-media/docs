IDML is a way for a page-layout document to travel as **text** rather than as an
opaque binary. A layout application can save its work in a packed binary file that
only that application fully understands; IDML is the interchange form of the same
document, where the structure is written out as XML and bundled into a single
archive.

Two ideas are worth holding onto from the start, because everything else follows
from them.

**It is a package, not a file.** What looks like one `.idml` document is really a
ZIP archive containing many small XML parts — a manifest that names the others,
parts that describe shared resources like colors and styles, and parts that hold
the actual pages and text. Open the archive and you can read every part with a
text editor.

**It describes content and intent, not pixels.** An IDML part does not say "put a
black dot at this coordinate." It says "this story uses this paragraph style, and
flows through this frame on this page." Turning that intent into pixels is the job
of a renderer — which is what Paged does, and what much of this reference is
secretly about. When we explain a part of the format, we explain it from what our
renderer actually has to do with it.

That is the whole shape of the thing: an archive of XML, describing a document's
structure and intent, meant to be read and rewritten by tools rather than stared
at by people. The next pages explain where this form came from and why it is
packaged the way it is.
