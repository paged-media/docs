**In short:** `paged.slide` makes presentations a document type. A PowerPoint
`.pptx` opens as native pages: one slide per page, layouts as master spreads,
shapes, charts, tables and SmartArt as editable native content, and speaker notes
kept with each slide. PowerPoint itself is the reference: geometry, baselines,
chart bars and table styles are checked against what it draws. The editor loads
it; its npm package is not published yet, and parts of it are still in
development.

## What it does

- Reads `.pptx`, `.ppsx` and `.potx`: masters, layouts, themes, every preset and
  custom shape, text, pictures, charts, tables (including PowerPoint's built-in
  table styles) and SmartArt drawings.
- Resolves PowerPoint's inheritance (theme, master, layout, slide) and writes the
  deck as a native document, a page per slide.
- A slide sorter (reorder, duplicate, delete, hide) and a speaker-notes panel; a
  new slide from a layout.
- In development: export back to `.pptx` (slide order, hidden slides and notes
  first; edited slide content next), and a slideshow with a presenter view.

## How it's built

A Cargo workspace (`pptx-core`, `pptx-import`, `slide-resolve`, `slide-geom`,
`slide-chart`, `slide-idml`, `pptx-export`) over the shared OOXML package layer,
plus a TS bundle (`@paged-media/slide`). Scripts drive PowerPoint to record the
answers the tests compare against.
