**In short:** `paged.slide` makes presentations a document type. A PowerPoint
`.pptx` opens as native pages: one slide per page, layouts as master spreads,
shapes, charts, tables and SmartArt as editable native content, and speaker notes
kept with each slide. PowerPoint itself is the reference: geometry, baselines,
chart bars and table styles are checked against what it draws. It is built into
the editor (the built-in modules are not published to npm), and parts of it are
still in development.

## What it does

- Reads `.pptx`, `.ppsx` and `.potx`: masters, layouts, themes, every preset and
  custom shape, text, pictures, charts, tables (including PowerPoint's built-in
  table styles) and SmartArt drawings.
- Resolves PowerPoint's inheritance (theme, master, layout, slide) and writes the
  deck as a native document, a page per slide.
- A slide sorter (reorder, duplicate, delete, hide, sections) and a
  speaker-notes panel; a new slide from a layout. Slides, layouts and
  placeholders are objects that scripts and panels read and write (notes, the
  hidden flag, the transition).
- Fonts the deck embeds are used when their licence allows installing or
  editing them; restricted and preview-only faces are named, not used.
- Export back to `.pptx` from the original deck and the edited document: a
  slide the document did not change keeps its bytes, an edited slide is its
  original XML with only the edited shapes rewritten, and shapes and slides made
  in the editor are written whole. What cannot round-trip yet (effect changes,
  edits inside SmartArt, added table rows, and others) is listed one line per
  shape and written as the deck had it. Checked by PowerPoint opening the written
  deck without a repair prompt; an unedited document exports the original bytes.
- A slide show (from the beginning or the current slide) and a presenter view,
  with PowerPoint's transitions drawn as WebGPU shaders.
- Animations (experimental): a slide's PowerPoint timeline is read, played in
  the show, previewed on the canvas, edited in the Animations pane (add, remove,
  timing, order, the Animation Painter, motion paths) and written back.
- Output (experimental): **File ▸ Export…** writes a PDF of the slides, of
  handouts or of notes pages, the outline as RTF or text, and an MP4 video with
  the transitions and animations playing; **Save as template** writes `.potx`;
  custom shows and sections are defined and kept in the deck. Every output runs
  headless too, except the video, which needs a browser's video encoder.
- Typed commands for scripts and the command line — slides, import and export,
  animations, every output, custom shows, sections, layouts and themes — and
  palette commands with menu entries; an opened deck exports under its own name
  ([paged.slide commands](/docs/paged/commands/slide)).
- In development: exporting what the native model cannot say yet (listed
  above), new masters and layouts, vector handouts and notes pages (they are
  150 dpi images today).

## How it's built

Rust crates in the engine's Cargo workspace (`pptx-core`, `pptx-import`,
`pptx-export`, `slide-resolve`, `slide-chart`, `slide-native`, `slide-anim`,
`slide-output`, `slide-shaders`, `slide-sheet`, `slide-js`) over the shared
OOXML layers in `platform/shared` (`paged-ooxml` for the package, `dml-geom` for
preset geometry, `dml-chart` and `dml-native`), plus a TS bundle
(`@paged-media/slide`). Scripts drive PowerPoint to record the answers the tests
compare against.
