**Spacing inside a line comes from tracking and kerning; spacing between lines comes from leading.**

**In short:** `Tracking` adds the same space after every character of a run, in thousandths of
an em. Kerning adjusts the space between particular pairs of characters: `KerningMethod` chooses
how it is computed (InDesign writes `$ID/Metrics`, `$ID/Optical` or `$ID/None`), and
`KerningValue` adds manual kerning. `Leading` is the distance from one baseline to the next, in
points or `Auto`, which is the point size times the paragraph's `AutoLeading` percentage.
`BaselineShift` moves glyphs up or down, and `HorizontalScale`, `VerticalScale` and `Skew`
distort them.

## Tracking

`Tracking` is in thousandths of an em, so its effect scales with the type size: at 12 pt, a
`Tracking` of `100` adds 1.2 pt after each character, and a negative value tightens the run. It is
a character attribute, set on a range or a style.

## Kerning

Kerning is the space between two specific characters, such as the tighter fit of "To" or "AV".
`KerningMethod` chooses where automatic kerning comes from, and InDesign writes its values in the
reserved `$ID/` namespace:

| Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `$ID/Metrics` | Use the kerning pairs built into the font. InDesign's default. |
| `$ID/Optical` | Compute the spacing from the shapes of adjacent glyphs, ignoring the font's pairs. Useful for fonts with few or no kerning pairs, and between characters of different fonts. |
| `$ID/None` | No automatic kerning. |

The census of real documents shows the prefixed spelling throughout: `$ID/Metrics` on paragraph
styles in 271 documents and `$ID/Optical` in 8, and `$ID/Optical` on runs. No bare `Metrics`
or `Optical` appears among the recorded values.

`KerningValue` is manual kerning: an amount in thousandths of an em added to the automatic
kerning at that position, set on the range it applies to.

> **In Paged:** kerning is applied by the shaper from the font's pairs. The `$ID/`-prefixed
> values are not recognised yet: every one of them, `$ID/None` included, falls back to metric
> kerning. The bare value `None` does turn kerning off, and optical kerning is not implemented
> (it is set like metric kerning). `KerningValue` is not read yet. These are known engine
> issues. <SupportBadge feature="typography.tracking-kerning" />

## Leading

Leading is the distance from one line's baseline to the next. InDesign treats it as a character
attribute: each line takes the largest leading among the runs on it. On a range or a style it is a
typed `<Properties>` child:

- `<Leading type="unit">14</Leading>` — 14 pt;
- `<Leading type="enumeration">Auto</Leading>` — auto leading.

Auto leading is the point size times the paragraph's `AutoLeading` percentage, 120 by default. A
line with mixed sizes gets the auto leading of its largest size. `AutoLeading` is a paragraph
attribute, so different paragraphs can use different percentages.

> **In Paged:** leading in points is applied, from either the typed child or an attribute. Auto
> leading uses 120 % of the size; a paragraph's own `AutoLeading` percentage is not read yet.
> <SupportBadge feature="typography.leading" />

## Baseline shift, scaling and skew

`BaselineShift` moves the glyphs off the baseline by that many points, up for positive values,
without changing the leading. `HorizontalScale` and `VerticalScale` stretch the glyphs as a
percentage (100 is unchanged). `Skew` slants them by that many degrees, a false italic.

> **In Paged:** baseline shift, both scales and skew are applied.

## Frequently asked questions

**How does InDesign write `KerningMethod` values?**
With the `$ID/` prefix: `$ID/Metrics`, `$ID/Optical` and `$ID/None`. A reader should accept that
spelling; a writer should produce it.

**What is the difference between metric and optical kerning?**
Metric kerning uses the kerning pairs the font designer built into the font. Optical kerning
ignores them and computes the spacing from the shapes of neighbouring glyphs. None turns automatic
kerning off.

**What unit is `Tracking` in?**
Thousandths of an em, so it scales with the type size: 100 at 12 pt is 1.2 pt per character.

**How is auto leading computed?**
The point size times the paragraph's `AutoLeading` percentage, 120 by default. On a line with
mixed sizes, the largest size decides.
