**A drop cap enlarges a paragraph's first characters down several lines; a tab moves the text after it to the next tab stop in the paragraph's `TabList`.**

**In short:** A drop cap is two paragraph attributes: `DropCapCharacters` (how many characters)
and `DropCapLines` (how many lines tall). The text stays in `Content` unchanged. A tab is the
character U+0009 inside `<Content>`, usually written `&#9;`; there is no tab element. Where it
moves the text is set by the paragraph's tab stops: a `<TabList>` of typed `<ListItem>` records
inside the paragraph's `<Properties>`, each with an `Alignment`, an `AlignmentCharacter`, a
`Leader` and a `Position`. Past the last stop, tabs fall on a default grid.

## Drop caps

<AttrTable element="ParagraphStyle" attrs={['DropCapCharacters', 'DropCapLines']} />

`DropCapCharacters` is the number of characters at the start of the paragraph that drop, and
`DropCapLines` the number of lines they span; `0` for either means no drop cap. The drop cap is
scaled so its top aligns with the cap height of the first line and its baseline with the baseline
of the last line it spans, and the lines beside it are indented around it. The characters remain
part of the text: a drop cap is not a separate run or frame.

InDesign can also give the drop cap a character style of its own, chosen with the drop cap
settings, so that the large letter takes a different font or colour. No document in the census
uses one.

</ExampleEmbed>

> **In Paged:** drop caps are set from `DropCapCharacters` and `DropCapLines`, on the paragraph
> or its style. `DropcapDetail`, as InDesign spells it (the older `DropCapDetail` spelling reads
> too), is read and kept as an integer. A drop cap's own character style, which InDesign writes
> as the paragraph's first nested style with the `Dropcap` delimiter, is applied to the cap;
> without one the cap takes the formatting of the characters it is made of.

## The tab character

A tab is U+0009 inside `<Content>`. XML allows a literal tab character, but writers usually
escape it as `&#9;` so that it survives editors and formatters. There is no tab element: the
character itself moves the text after it to the next tab stop.

## Tab stops

A paragraph's tab stops are a typed list in its `<Properties>` block, on a
`<ParagraphStyleRange>` or a paragraph style. Each stop is a `<ListItem type="record">` with four
typed children:

| Child | Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `<Alignment>` | `enumeration` | `LeftAlign`, `CenterAlign`, `RightAlign` or `CharacterAlign` |
| `<AlignmentCharacter>` | `string` | for `CharacterAlign`, the character that lines up on the stop, usually `.` |
| `<Leader>` | `string` | characters repeated across the gap the tab opens, such as `.` or `. `; empty for none |
| `<Position>` | `unit` | the stop's position in points from the left edge of the text column |

The alignments place the text after the tab:

- `LeftAlign` — the text starts at the stop.
- `RightAlign` — the text ends at the stop.
- `CenterAlign` — the text is centred on the stop.
- `CharacterAlign` — the first occurrence of the alignment character lands on the stop, which
  lines up a column of decimal numbers.

When the text is already past the last stop, or the paragraph has none, each further tab moves to
the next position on a default grid of every 36 pt (half an inch). In a paragraph with a hanging
indent (a positive `LeftIndent` and a negative `FirstLineIndent`), the left indent acts as a stop,
so a tab after a list number lands on the text edge.

<ExampleEmbed example="tab-stops">
  A table-of-contents line: a left stop at 144 pt, then a right stop at 432 pt with a dot leader.
  Each `&#9;` in the Content moves the text after it to the next stop.
</ExampleEmbed>

> **In Paged:** tab stops are honoured with all four alignments, leaders and the hanging-indent
> stop; leaders are drawn as whole copies of the leader string. A `RightAlign` or `CenterAlign`
> segment that would collide with the text before it falls back to left alignment for that tab.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is a tab written in IDML?**
As the character U+0009 inside `<Content>`, normally escaped as `&#9;`. There is no separate tab
element.

**How are tab stops written?**
As `<Properties><TabList type="list">` on the paragraph or its style, with one
`<ListItem type="record">` per stop holding typed `<Alignment>`, `<AlignmentCharacter>`,
`<Leader>` and `<Position>` children.

**Where do tabs go when there are no tab stops?**
To a default grid every 36 pt, measured from the left edge of the column.

**How do I make a drop cap in IDML?**
Set `DropCapCharacters` and `DropCapLines` on the paragraph or its style, for example `1` and `3`
for a single letter three lines tall. The text itself does not change.
