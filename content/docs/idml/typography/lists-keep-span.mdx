**Three paragraph-level features that change where text goes: generated list markers, rules that keep lines and paragraphs together across frame breaks, and paragraphs that span or split the columns of a frame.**

**In short:** A paragraph becomes a list item through `BulletsAndNumberingListType`
(`BulletList` or `NumberedList`); the bullet or number is generated, never stored in `Content`.
Numbers follow `NumberingExpression` and the format in a typed `<NumberingFormat>` child, and a
`<NumberingList>` can carry one sequence across stories. Keep options (`KeepWithNext`,
`KeepLinesTogether` and its companions) and `StartParagraph` decide where a paragraph may break
between frames and columns. `SpanColumnType` lets a paragraph span several columns of a frame,
such as a headline over a two-column article, or split its column into narrower ones.

## Bullets and numbering

<ExampleEmbed example="numbered-list">
  Three list items numbered 1, 2 and 3. The numbers come from the list attributes on the range;
  the Content holds only the item text.
</ExampleEmbed>

The list attributes sit on a paragraph style or a `<ParagraphStyleRange>`. Most are attributes;
InDesign writes a few as typed `<Properties>` children and ignores them as attributes.

| Name | Spelled as | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `BulletsAndNumberingListType` | attribute | `NoList`, `BulletList` or `NumberedList` |
| `NumberingExpression` | attribute | the number's text, in which `^#` stands for the number and `^t` for a tab. Default `^#.^t` |
| `NumberingStartAt` | attribute | the first number of the list |
| `NumberingContinue` | attribute | `false` restarts the count at this paragraph |
| `NumberingLevel` | attribute | the level in a multi-level list |
| `BulletsTextAfter` | attribute | the text after a bullet, by default a tab (`^t`) |
| `<NumberingFormat>` | `string` child | the number style: `1, 2, 3, 4...`, `I, II, III, IV...`, `a, b, c, d...` and so on |
| `<BulletChar>` | child with attributes | the bullet: `BulletCharacterType` and `BulletCharacterValue` (the Unicode code point, in decimal) |
| `<AppliedNumberingList>` | `object` child | the `NumberingList` the paragraph belongs to |
| `<BulletsCharacterStyle>`, `<NumberingCharacterStyle>` | `object` children | the character style for the bullet or number |

A bullet is written, for example, as `<BulletChar BulletCharacterType="UnicodeOnly"
BulletCharacterValue="8226"/>` inside `<Properties>`: U+2022, the bullet character.

Numbering counts consecutive numbered paragraphs within a story. A named `<NumberingList>`, a
child of `<Document>` in `designmap.xml`, can carry the count across stories
(`ContinueNumbersAcrossStories`) and across the documents of a book
(`ContinueNumbersAcrossDocuments`). Paragraphs join it through `<AppliedNumberingList>`.

> **In Paged:** bullets and numbers are generated from the paragraph's list attributes with the
> numbering formats InDesign offers, and the text after the marker lands on the hanging indent.
> `<NumberingFormat>`, `<AppliedNumberingList>` and the marker character styles are read as the
> typed children InDesign writes. `<NumberingList>` definitions are read only from
> `Resources/Styles.xml`, not from `designmap.xml` where InDesign writes them, so numbering
> continues across stories only for lists defined there. Multi-level numbering
> (`NumberingLevel`) is not read yet. <SupportBadge feature="styles.bullets-numbering" />

## Keep options

Keep options say which lines must share a frame or column, so that a break falls elsewhere.

| Attribute | Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `KeepWithNext` | integer | the number of lines of the next paragraph that must be in the same frame or column as this paragraph's last line; `0` is off. Typical for headings. |
| `KeepLinesTogether` | boolean | switches the within-paragraph rules below on |
| `KeepAllLinesTogether` | boolean | with `KeepLinesTogether`, the whole paragraph moves rather than breaks |
| `KeepFirstLines` | integer | with `KeepLinesTogether`, at least this many lines before a break |
| `KeepLastLines` | integer | with `KeepLinesTogether`, at least this many lines after a break |
| `StartParagraph` | enumeration | where the paragraph starts: `Anywhere`, `NextColumn`, `NextFrame`, `NextPage`, `NextOddPage`, `NextEvenPage` |

InDesign applies these by moving lines. With `KeepWithNext`, a paragraph's last line moves to join
the next paragraph, not necessarily the whole paragraph. With `KeepFirstLines`, a paragraph that
would leave too few lines before the break moves entirely; with `KeepLastLines`, lines are pulled
over until enough follow the break.

> **In Paged:** all six are honoured, following the line-moving behaviour measured against
> InDesign. A break is only forced where a next frame or column exists.

## Span and split columns

In a multi-column frame, a paragraph can leave the column grid:

| Name | Spelled as | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SpanColumnType` | attribute | `SingleColumn` (normal), `SpanColumns` or `SplitColumns` |
| `<SpanSplitColumnCount>` | `short` child, or `enumeration` `All` | how many columns a span covers, or how many sub-columns a split makes |
| `SpanColumnMinSpaceBefore`, `SpanColumnMinSpaceAfter` | attributes, points | minimum space above and below a span or split |
| `SplitColumnInsideGutter`, `SplitColumnOutsideGutter` | attributes, points | the gutters between sub-columns, and at their outer edges |

A spanning paragraph runs across the given number of columns. The text before it, from the top of
its band, is balanced over those columns by line count, and the text after it continues below it.
A split paragraph divides its own column into narrower sub-columns, balanced by line count; a run
of consecutive split paragraphs with the same settings forms one block.

> **In Paged:** spanning and splitting are laid out, including the balancing above a span and the
> block rules between consecutive split paragraphs, as measured against InDesign.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are list numbers stored in the IDML text?**
No. The number or bullet is generated from the paragraph's list attributes when the text is laid
out. `Content` holds only the item text.

**How is a bullet character written?**
As `<BulletChar>` inside the paragraph's or style's `<Properties>`, with `BulletCharacterType` and
`BulletCharacterValue`, the code point in decimal (8226 for •).

**What is the difference between `KeepWithNext` and `KeepLinesTogether`?**
`KeepWithNext` ties a paragraph to the one after it, which keeps a heading with its text.
`KeepLinesTogether` controls breaks inside a paragraph, together with `KeepAllLinesTogether`,
`KeepFirstLines` and `KeepLastLines`.

**How do I make a headline span two columns?**
Set `SpanColumnType="SpanColumns"` on the headline's paragraph and give the count as
`<Properties><SpanSplitColumnCount type="short">2</SpanSplitColumnCount></Properties>`, or
`type="enumeration"` with `All` for every column.
