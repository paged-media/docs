## How to read the plate

The plate is page 1 of the example `based-on-chain`, as InDesign exports it. The outline is
its one text frame, many times taller than the single line its story fills. That gap is the
point of the guide. A frame is geometry on a spread; the words belong to a story in a
part of their own; how they are set comes from a paragraph style, and from the styles it is
based on.

Each of the five stops is one element of the package, with the attributes this example gives
it. Its reference entry lists every attribute the element takes, the documents a census found
it in, and the examples that use it.

- [Package anatomy](/docs/idml/package-anatomy): the design map and the parts it names.
- [Geometry and coordinates](/docs/idml/geometry-coordinates): spreads, transforms and bounds.
- [Story structure](/docs/idml/stories-text/story-structure): how a story holds its text.
- [The cascade](/docs/idml/styles/the-cascade): how a style chain resolves, with this example.
