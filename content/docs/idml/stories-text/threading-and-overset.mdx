**Threading is how one story flows through a chain of frames; overset is the text that still doesn't fit at the end.**

**In short:** A story holds text and a frame shows it, so a story longer than its frame
has to go somewhere. IDML's answer is *threading*: frames link into a chain with
`NextTextFrame` and `PreviousTextFrame`, and the story fills them in order. Text that
still doesn't fit at the end of the chain is *overset*. Overset is an outcome of layout,
not something the file records: the text stays in the story, and the page simply doesn't
show it.

## A story is independent of its frames

The text lives in one `Stories/Story_*.xml` part however many frames display it. Frames
don't contain text; they name a story in their `ParentStory` attribute and contribute a
box for it to flow into. "One story in three frames" is one `Story` part and three
`TextFrame` elements with the same `ParentStory`. The story is composed as one continuous
stream and poured into the frames in chain order.

## The chain: `NextTextFrame` and `PreviousTextFrame`

Each frame in a thread names the following frame's `Self` id in `NextTextFrame` and the
preceding one in `PreviousTextFrame`. The first frame of a chain has
`PreviousTextFrame="n"` and the last has `NextTextFrame="n"`; `n` is IDML's "none". The
head of a chain is therefore the frame that no other frame names as its next one.

A chain can cross pages and spreads: the frames are ordinary page items on whichever
spreads they sit, and only the links say how the text runs.

## Columns inside one frame

A frame with several columns (`TextColumnCount` in its `TextFramePreference`) fills them
in order before the text moves on to the next frame. With `VerticalBalanceColumns` set,
InDesign balances the columns by **line count**: each column is capped at the total number
of lines divided by the number of columns, rounded up. The columns do not come out at equal
heights when their lines differ in height. See [text frames](/docs/idml/frames-paths/text-frames).

## Overset: the text that doesn't fit

When the composed story needs more room than the chain provides, the remaining lines are
overset. InDesign shows a red plus sign in the last frame's out-port; the IDML carries
nothing for it. The overset text is still in the story part, so anything that reads the
story (see [extract all text](/docs/idml/stories-text/extract-all-text)) gets all of it;
only the printed page leaves it out.

A frame can avoid overset by growing instead: `AutoSizingType` in its
`TextFramePreference` lets the frame's height (or width) follow its text.

> **In Paged:** the last frame clips its overset lines the way InDesign's own PDF does, and
> the load reports a warning that names the story. Auto-sizing frames grow to fit.

## Text that never flows

Some text is in the story but not part of the printed text at all:

- **Conditional text** whose condition is hidden. Conditions and their visibility are
  covered in [conditional text](/docs/idml/conditional-text).
- **Notes** (`Note`) and **hidden text**, which are editorial content attached to the
  text. <SupportBadge feature="stories-text.hidden-text" />

Unlike overset text, these runs are left out because of what they are, not because of
where they ended up.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is text threading in IDML?**
Threading is how one story flows through more than one frame. Each frame names the next
with `NextTextFrame` and the previous with `PreviousTextFrame`, forming a chain that the
story fills in order.

**What is overset text?**
The text that still doesn't fit once the last frame in the chain is full. It is a result
of layout, not an attribute: InDesign marks it with a red plus sign, and the IDML records
nothing.

**Is overset text lost?**
No. It stays in the story part; only the printed page omits it.

**Do all the frames that show a story hold a copy of the text?**
No. The text lives in one story part; frames only reference it through `ParentStory`.
