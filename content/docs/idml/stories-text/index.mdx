**In IDML, all of a document's text lives in stories, separate from the frames that show it.**

**In short:** A *story* is IDML's container for one stream of text. The characters are
grouped into runs of shared formatting: `ParagraphStyleRange` elements for paragraph
formatting and, inside them, `CharacterStyleRange` elements for character formatting.
Paragraphs are not elements; each one ends with a `<Br/>` mark in the text. Frames on
pages *display* a story rather than containing it, so one story can thread through
several frames, and text that doesn't fit is *overset*. This section explains how a story
is built, how it flows, and how to read its text back out.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a story in IDML?**
A story is IDML's container for one stream of text. It lives in its own
`Stories/Story_*.xml` part and is referenced by the frames that display it, rather than
living inside any one frame.

**How is the text in a story structured?**
As two layers of runs over the characters: `ParagraphStyleRange` elements for stretches
that share paragraph formatting, `CharacterStyleRange` elements inside them for stretches
that share character formatting, and the characters in `Content`. A `<Br/>` in the text
ends each paragraph except the last. See [story structure](/docs/idml/stories-text/story-structure).

**Can one story appear in more than one frame?**
Yes. A story can *thread* through a chain of frames, filling them in order; text that
still doesn't fit at the end of the chain is overset. See
[threading and overset](/docs/idml/stories-text/threading-and-overset).
