**The content parts, `Spreads`, `MasterSpreads` and `Stories`, hold the document itself: its pages, the items on them, and the text.**

**In short:** A spread part holds one `<Spread>`: its pages and every page item placed
across them, in the spread's own coordinate space. A master spread part has the same shape
and holds the template pages body pages inherit from. A story part holds one `<Story>`:
text and formatting with no geometry, as runs of paragraph and character attributes with
the `<Br/>` paragraph mark in between. A text frame on a spread links the two by naming
its story in `ParentStory`.

The design map lists these parts with `idPkg:Spread`, `idPkg:MasterSpread` and
`idPkg:Story` elements. The package below has one spread with two frames and two stories:

## `Spreads/Spread_<id>.xml`: pages and the items on them

A spread part holds one `<Spread>`. Its children are the spread's `<Page>` elements, then
its page items: `<TextFrame>`, `<Rectangle>`, `<Oval>`, `<Polygon>`, `<GraphicLine>` and
`<Group>`, in stacking order from back to front.

Page items are not children of a page. They sit in the spread's coordinate space, and each
`<Page>` is placed in that space by its own `ItemTransform`. In a facing spread the spine
is at x = 0, so the left page sits at negative x. Which page an item belongs to is a
matter of where it lies. The coordinate model is covered in
[geometry and coordinates](/docs/idml/geometry-coordinates).

The attributes InDesign needs on every spread (`PageCount`, `BindingLocation`, an
`ItemTransform` on the spread, on each page and on each item) are listed in
[what InDesign needs to open a file](/docs/idml/package-anatomy/what-indesign-requires).

### Pages

A `<Page>` gives its size as `GeometricBounds`, the master it applies as `AppliedMaster`,
the master items it has overridden as `OverrideList`, and its label as `Name` (the page
number as InDesign displayed it: "1", "iii", "A-3"). Its children hold the page's margins
and columns (`MarginPreference`), its ruler guides (`Guide`) and typed properties such as
`PageColor`. The details are in [spreads and pages](/docs/idml/layout-model/spreads-and-pages).

### Page items

Every page item has a `Self` id, an `ItemTransform` and its outline in
`<Properties><PathGeometry>`. Most also carry a `Name` (the name shown in the Layers
panel), `ItemLayer` (the layer it sits on), `Visible` and `Locked`, and an
`AppliedObjectStyle`. A text frame adds the text links:

- `ParentStory` names the story whose text flows into the frame;
- `PreviousTextFrame` and `NextTextFrame` name the frames before and after it in the
  thread, or `n` at either end;
- a `TextFramePreference` child holds its columns, insets and auto-sizing.

A rectangle, oval or polygon can contain another item, as InDesign's Paste Into does: an
image, a group, or a text frame, clipped by the host's path. The element types are covered
in [page item types](/docs/idml/frames-paths/page-item-types) and
[text frames](/docs/idml/frames-paths/text-frames).

> **In Paged:** text frames inside groups and inside paste-into hosts are read, with their
> transforms composed, and are clipped by the host. A group pasted into a host is not yet
> read. <SupportBadge feature="frames-paths.nested-content" />

## `MasterSpreads/MasterSpread_<id>.xml`: master pages

A master spread has the same shape as a spread: one `<MasterSpread>` with `<Page>`
children and page items. Body pages apply it by naming its id in `AppliedMaster`, and its
items appear on every page that applies it unless the page overrides them. A master spread
also records its name three ways: `NamePrefix` ("A"), `BaseName` ("Master") and the `Name`
they compose ("A-Master"). Masters are covered in
[master spreads and overrides](/docs/idml/master-spreads-overrides).

## `Stories/Story_<id>.xml`: the text

A story part holds one `<Story>`: the text and its formatting, with no geometry. Its
content is a flat sequence of `<ParagraphStyleRange>` elements, each holding
`<CharacterStyleRange>` elements, which hold the characters:

- a **`ParagraphStyleRange`** is a run of one or more paragraphs that share their paragraph
  attributes, including `AppliedParagraphStyle`;
- a **`CharacterStyleRange`** is a run of characters that share their character attributes,
  including `AppliedCharacterStyle`; the font is a typed child,
  `<Properties><AppliedFont type="string">`;
- **`Content`** holds the characters themselves;
- **`<Br/>`** is the paragraph mark: it ends every paragraph except the story's last,
  inside the last character range of that paragraph;
- a forced line break is the character U+2028 and a tab is U+0009, both inside `Content`.

A new `ParagraphStyleRange` does not start a new paragraph by itself; only a `<Br/>` does.
See [the paragraph model](/docs/idml/stories-text/the-paragraph-model) and
[story structure](/docs/idml/stories-text/story-structure).

A story's own settings, such as its orientation for vertical CJK text, are in a
`StoryPreference` child of `<Story>`.

> **In Paged:** stories are read as paragraph and character runs, with tables, anchored
> objects and markers in place. <SupportBadge feature="package-anatomy.story-parts" />

## How the ids tie back to the design map

The design map's `StoryList` lists the story ids; each has an `idPkg:Story` whose `src`
names its part, and the part's `<Story Self="…">` carries the same id. The id appears once
more as `ParentStory` on each frame the story flows through. Listed in the manifest, named
on a frame, carried on the story element: that triple places a story's text on a page.
InDesign discards a story that no frame references when it opens the file.
Following ids from part to part is the subject of
[how the parts reference each other](/docs/idml/package-anatomy/how-parts-reference).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between a spread, a master spread and a story?**
A spread holds pages and the page items placed across them. A master spread holds the
template pages and items that body pages inherit, in the same shape. A story holds text and
its formatting with no geometry; a text frame on a spread links to it through
`ParentStory`.

**Are page items children of a `Page`?**
No. They are children of the `Spread`, positioned in the spread's coordinate space. Pages
are placed in the same space by their own `ItemTransform`, and an item belongs to the page
it lies on.

**How does a `TextFrame` connect to the text it displays?**
It names a story in `ParentStory`. The story part holds the text; the frame supplies the
area it is composed into, and `NextTextFrame` names the frame the text continues in.

**Is `<Br/>` a line break?**
No. `<Br/>` is the paragraph mark: it ends a paragraph. A line break that stays inside the
paragraph is the character U+2028 in `Content`.

**What happens to a story that no frame uses?**
InDesign drops it when it opens the file. A story only stays in the document while a frame
(or an anchored frame inside another story) places it.
