**A table of contents is an ordinary story that InDesign generated; a `TOCStyle` records the recipe it was generated from.**

**In short:** The recipe is a `TOCStyle` in `Resources/Styles.xml`: a title, the
paragraph style for the title, and one `TOCStyleEntry` per paragraph style to collect.
Each entry names the style to collect (`IncludeStyle`), the style to format the entry
with (`FormatStyle`), an outline `Level`, and how to place the page number. The result
is a normal story of styled paragraphs with the page numbers typed out, and the story
names the recipe in its `AppliedTOCStyle` attribute. Nothing in the file updates the
list when pages move; regenerating it is the application's job.

## The recipe: `TOCStyle`

A `TOCStyle` is a direct child of the `idPkg:Styles` root, with no group element around
it. InDesign writes a default one into every document: 271 documents in the census carry
one, almost all of them without entries. A document that has a real table of contents
adds a style with entries.

- `Title` is the heading text, and `TitleStyle` the paragraph style it is set in.
- `IncludeHidden` decides whether paragraphs on hidden layers count;
  `IncludeBookDocuments` extends the collection to the other documents of a book.
- `RunIn` sets all entries of a level in one paragraph instead of one per line.
- `CreateBookmarks` turns the entries into PDF bookmarks on export, and `MakeAnchor`
  places a text anchor at each source paragraph.
- `RemoveForcedLineBreak` strips forced line breaks from the collected text, and
  `NumberedParagraphs` decides whether a list number is kept.

## The entries: `TOCStyleEntry`

Each `TOCStyleEntry` child of a `TOCStyle` collects the paragraphs in one paragraph
style:

- `IncludeStyle` is the paragraph style to collect, for example a "Heading 1".
- `FormatStyle` is the paragraph style the entry is set in. InDesign writes it as an
  attribute or as a typed `Properties` child.
- `Level` is the outline depth, 1 for the top level.
- `PageNumber` and `PageNumberPosition` say whether the entry carries a page number and
  where; `Separator` is the text between the entry and the number, and
  `SeparatorStyle` and `PageNumberStyle` are the character styles for the two.
- `SortAlphabet` sorts the entries of a level alphabetically instead of in page order.

Entries are rare in real files: only three documents in the census have any.

## The result: a generated story

Generating a table of contents walks the document's pages, picks every paragraph whose
style is an entry's `IncludeStyle`, and writes one paragraph per match, set in the
entry's `FormatStyle`, followed by the separator and the page number. The title comes
first, in `TitleStyle`. The output is placed in a text frame like any other story.

In the file, that story is ordinary text: `ParagraphStyleRange`s and
`CharacterStyleRange`s with the entries and page numbers as `Content`. What marks it as
a table of contents is the `Story` element's `AppliedTOCStyle` attribute, which names
the `TOCStyle` it came from. Every other story says `AppliedTOCStyle="n"`. In the census,
91 documents write the attribute and one of them points it at a TOC style.

Because the page numbers are typed out, a table of contents goes stale when text moves
to other pages, until it is regenerated.

> **In Paged:** a table of contents prints the text its story holds, stale or not, as
> InDesign does until the table is updated. Only an empty story whose text frame names a TOC
> style in `AppliedTOCStyle` is filled with a fresh list: entries in story order, each with
> the label of the page its story starts on (section numbering included), set in the entry's
> `FormatStyle`; the title paragraph and `RunIn` are not applied there yet. The attribute on
> `Story`, where InDesign writes it, is not read yet.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where is a table of contents defined in IDML?**
The recipe is a `TOCStyle` with `TOCStyleEntry` children in `Resources/Styles.xml`. The
table itself is an ordinary story elsewhere in the package.

**How do I find the table-of-contents story in a package?**
Look for a `Story` whose `AppliedTOCStyle` names a `TOCStyle`. Other stories carry
`AppliedTOCStyle="n"`.

**Are the page numbers in a table of contents live?**
No. They are saved as text when the table is generated and are only correct until the
pages change.

**What does `IncludeStyle` mean?**
It names the paragraph style whose paragraphs the entry collects. `FormatStyle` names
the style the collected entry is then set in.

**Why does every document have a `TOCStyle`?**
InDesign writes a default TOC style into every document, whether or not it contains a
table of contents.
