**A reference in IDML is a pair: a record in the design map and an anchor in a story, joined by id.**

**In short:** IDML has five related kinds of reference. Hyperlinks and bookmarks
point somewhere and leave the text alone. Cross-references, index entries and a table
of contents are text that InDesign *generates* from other places in the document and
saves as ordinary text. The records (`Hyperlink`, destinations, `Bookmark`,
`CrossReferenceFormat`, `Topic`) sit in `designmap.xml`, after the last story
reference. The anchors (a linked stretch of text, a text destination, an index marker)
sit inside the stories. TOC styles live in `Resources/Styles.xml`.

## Five kinds of reference

| Kind | What it is | Where the parts live |
|---|---|---|
| Hyperlink | a source (text, or a page item) that jumps to a URL, a page or a text anchor | `Hyperlink` and the destination in the design map; `HyperlinkTextSource` in the story |
| Bookmark | a named entry in a PDF's navigation pane, with no source in the text | `Bookmark` in the design map |
| Cross-reference | a source whose words are built from its target ("see page 12") | `CrossReferenceSource` in the story; its `CrossReferenceFormat` and `Hyperlink` in the design map |
| Index entry | a point in the text that contributes a page to an index topic | `PageReference` markers in stories; `Topic` records |
| Table of contents | a story generated from paragraphs in chosen styles | `TOCStyle` in `Styles.xml`; the generated story names it in `AppliedTOCStyle` |

## Records and anchors

Each reference splits into a record and an anchor. The records are document-level
elements, direct children of `Document` in the design map. The anchors live where the
text is:

- a **text source** wraps the characters a reader clicks, inside the story;
- a **text destination** is an empty marker, `HyperlinkTextDestination`, at the spot a
  link or cross-reference lands on;
- an **index marker** is an empty `PageReference` at the spot that contributes a page.

The record names its anchor by `Self` id, so one destination can be reached from many
sources.

## Order matters in the design map

InDesign 20.0.1 binds hyperlinks and bookmarks only when the whole navigation block
(destinations, hyperlinks, bookmarks) comes after the last `idPkg:Story` reference. A
block placed earlier opens without error and binds nothing. The other rules InDesign
applies to these elements are collected in
[what InDesign requires to open a file](/docs/idml/package-anatomy/what-indesign-requires).

## Generated text is saved as text

Cross-references, an index and a table of contents are text that InDesign builds from
the rest of the document: a page number, the words of a heading, a list of entries.
IDML stores the result as ordinary `Content` in the story. A reader that does not
rebuild them sees the text as InDesign last generated it, which is right until the
pages move.

## Frequently asked questions

**What kinds of references does IDML have?**
Hyperlinks, bookmarks, cross-references, index entries and tables of contents. The
first two point somewhere; the last three produce text built from other parts of the
document.

**Where are hyperlinks stored in an IDML package?**
The `Hyperlink` element and its URL or page destination are in `designmap.xml`, after
the last `idPkg:Story` reference. The linked text is wrapped in a `HyperlinkTextSource`
inside the story.

**Why does InDesign ignore my hyperlinks?**
Usually because the navigation block sits before the story references, or because the
`Hyperlink` names its destination with an attribute instead of a typed
`<Properties><Destination>` child.

**Is a cross-reference's text stored in the file?**
Yes. The words InDesign generated are saved as ordinary text inside the
`CrossReferenceSource`, and the same is true of a generated index or table of contents.
