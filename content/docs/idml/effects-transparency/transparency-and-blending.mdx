**`<BlendingSetting>` sets how much of an object shows (`Opacity`) and how its colours combine with what is beneath (`BlendMode`).**

**In short:** `Opacity` is a percentage, 100 fully opaque. `BlendMode` is one of InDesign's
blend modes, `Normal` unless set. On a group, `IsolateBlending` keeps the members' blend modes
from reaching below the group and `KnockoutGroup` stops members from showing through each
other. The setting sits in one of four containers: `TransparencySetting` for the whole
object, or `FillTransparencySetting`, `StrokeTransparencySetting` or
`ContentTransparencySetting` for one part of it.

## `<BlendingSetting>`

<AttrTable element="BlendingSetting" />

InDesign's default, as its preferences write it: `BlendMode="Normal" Opacity="100"
KnockoutGroup="false" IsolateBlending="false"`.

### `Opacity`

A percentage from 0 (invisible) to 100 (opaque), written as a whole number in the surveyed
files. At the object level it fades everything the object draws together: fill, stroke,
content and effects. Opacity is not a tint: a 50 % tint is a lighter colour that still covers
what is beneath; 50 % opacity lets it show through.

### `BlendMode`

How the object's colours combine with the colours beneath it. The surveyed documents use
`Normal`, `Multiply`, `Screen`, `Overlay`, `SoftLight`, `Darken`, `Lighten` and `ColorBurn`,
`Multiply` most often after `Normal`. A blend mode affects everything beneath the object down
to the page, unless a group isolates it.

Blending happens in the document's blending space, `BlendingSpace` on the
`<TransparencyPreference>` in `Preferences.xml`: `CMYK` in nearly every surveyed file.

### `IsolateBlending` and `KnockoutGroup`

Both matter on a group:

- **`IsolateBlending="true"`**: the members' blend modes combine only with each other, not with
  what lies beneath the group.
- **`KnockoutGroup="true"`**: members do not show through one another; each member blends only
  with what is beneath the group.

Both are `false` in every surveyed file.

## The four levels

The same `<BlendingSetting>` can sit in four containers, written as children of the page item
after its `<Properties>`:

| Container | Opacity and blend mode apply to |
|---|---|
| `TransparencySetting` | the whole object |
| `FillTransparencySetting` | the fill only |
| `StrokeTransparencySetting` | the stroke only |
| `ContentTransparencySetting` | the content: the text of a text frame or the picture in a graphic frame |

A rectangle with a 30 % fill opacity and a fully opaque stroke is written as
`FillTransparencySetting` with `Opacity="30"`, while the object level stays at 100.
Object-level opacity multiplies with each part's own.

## Groups

A `<Group>`'s `TransparencySetting` treats the group as one: its members are composed first,
and the result is faded or blended as a whole. Three overlapping 50 %-opaque shapes look
different from the same shapes in a 50 %-opaque group: in the group they do not show through
each other.

> **In Paged:** object-level `Opacity` and `BlendMode` are applied to rectangles, ovals,
> polygons, text frames and groups; a group is composed as a unit. Paged also reads
> `HardLight`, `ColorDodge`, `Difference`, `Exclusion`, `Hue`, `Saturation`, `Color` and
> `Luminosity`. Opacity and blend mode on a graphic line are not applied yet, and
> `IsolateBlending` and `KnockoutGroup` are not read. The fill, stroke and content levels are
> not kept apart: an opacity or blend mode found there is applied to the whole object.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I make an object 50 % transparent in IDML?**
Give it `<TransparencySetting><BlendingSetting Opacity="50"/></TransparencySetting>` after its
`<Properties>`.

**What blend modes appear in real files?**
`Normal`, then `Multiply`, and also `Screen`, `Overlay`, `SoftLight`, `Darken`, `Lighten` and
`ColorBurn` in the surveyed documents.

**How do I fade only the fill and keep the stroke opaque?**
Put the `BlendingSetting` in `FillTransparencySetting` instead of `TransparencySetting`.

**Is opacity on a group the same as opacity on each member?**
No. A group's opacity applies to the composed group, so its members do not show through one
another.

**What do `IsolateBlending` and `KnockoutGroup` do?**
On a group, `IsolateBlending` confines the members' blend modes to the group and
`KnockoutGroup` keeps members from showing through each other.
