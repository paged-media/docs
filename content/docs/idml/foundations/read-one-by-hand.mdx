A few things to notice, top to bottom:

- The outer `idPkg:Story` is the part wrapper; the inner `Story` is the content,
  and its `Self` id is how the page's text frame finds it.
- A `ParagraphStyleRange` is a run of text sharing one set of paragraph attributes; a
  `CharacterStyleRange` nests inside for character-level formatting.
- The actual text sits in `Content`. It is ordinary XML text, so `&`, `<` and `>` are
  written as `&amp;`, `&lt;` and `&gt;`.

## When there is more than one paragraph

This story has one paragraph, so it shows nothing between paragraphs. Add a second and
you meet the one rule that trips up most hand-edited files: **a paragraph ends with a
`<Br/>` element**, a sibling of `Content` inside the last `CharacterStyleRange` of the
paragraph. Every paragraph except the story's last ends with one.

<ExampleEmbed example="paragraph-marks">
  Three paragraphs. The first two share one paragraph range and each ends with a
  `<Br/>`; the second holds a forced line break (`&#x2028;`). The third has its own
  range because its spacing differs.
</ExampleEmbed>

Three more characters are written the way InDesign writes them:

- **A new paragraph is `<Br/>`, not a new range.** A `ParagraphStyleRange` can hold
  several paragraphs. Writing one range per paragraph without the mark gives InDesign one
  long paragraph.
- **A forced line break is the character U+2028** inside `Content`. It breaks the line
  without ending the paragraph.
- **A tab is the character U+0009** inside `Content`, often written `&#9;`.

[The paragraph model](/docs/idml/stories-text/the-paragraph-model) explains why IDML
works this way.

That is the entire payload of the first document. Everything else in the package exists
to give the story a page to live on and styles to resolve against, which is what the
**[package anatomy](/docs/idml/package-anatomy)** section unpacks next.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a "story" in an IDML file?**
A story is the part that holds a run of text — the words themselves, grouped into
paragraphs and styled runs. The text frame on a page points at a story by its
`Self` id, so the page knows which words to flow into it.

**Can I edit IDML text in a plain text editor?**
Yes. The text lives in the `Content` element inside the story part, which is just
readable XML. To change what the page says, you edit the `Content` there. The
surrounding parts — manifest, resources, spread — give that story a page and styles
to resolve against.

**What is the difference between a `ParagraphStyleRange` and a `CharacterStyleRange`?**
A `ParagraphStyleRange` is a run of one or more paragraphs that share their paragraph
attributes. A `CharacterStyleRange` nests inside it for a stretch of characters that
share their character attributes. The text sits in `Content` elements inside the
`CharacterStyleRange`.

**How do I start a new paragraph when editing by hand?**
End the current paragraph with a `<Br/>` element after its last `Content`. Starting a new
`ParagraphStyleRange` alone does not end the paragraph: InDesign reads consecutive ranges
without a mark as one paragraph.
