**`<Color>` holds the channel values; `<Swatch>` is a named entry with no values of its own. Page items name either by its `Self` id.**

**In short:** The palette lives in `Resources/Graphic.xml`. A `<Color>` carries a `Model`
(process, spot or registration), a `Space` (`CMYK`, `RGB` or `LAB`) and a `ColorValue` with
the numbers for that space. A spot colour also carries an alternate. A `<Swatch>` is an
entry such as `Swatch/None`, the "no paint" swatch. Every document carries the same handful
of reserved colours, marked by `ColorOverride`. Named tints are a third element, `<Tint>`,
covered on [gradients & tints](/docs/idml/color-swatches/gradients-and-tints).

## `<Color>`

<AttrTable element="Color" />

The attributes that decide the colour are `Model`, `Space`, `ColorValue` and, for a spot
colour, `AlternateSpace` and `AlternateColorValue`. The rest is bookkeeping for the Swatches
panel: `Name`, `ColorEditable`, `ColorRemovable`, `Visible`, `SwatchCreatorID` and the colour
group a swatch belongs to.

### `Model`

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Process` | Printed as a mix of the process inks. The model of almost every colour in real files. |
| `Spot` | A named ink of its own, printed on its own plate. |
| `Registration` | The reserved `Color/Registration`, which prints on every plate. |

### `Space` and `ColorValue`

`Space` says how to read `ColorValue`, a space-separated list of numbers:

| `Space` | `ColorValue` | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `CMYK` | four percentages, C M Y K, each 0 to 100 | `0 100 100 0` |
| `RGB` | three components, R G B, each 0 to 255 | `255 0 0` |
| `LAB` | L from 0 to 100, then a and b, which can be negative | `46.27 20 33` |

InDesign spells the Lab space in capitals, `LAB`, and writes L with decimals. Of the 305
documents surveyed for this reference, every one has CMYK colours, 77 have RGB colours and
3 have Lab colours.

### Spot colours and their alternate

A spot colour describes an ink that is not made from the process inks. Besides its own
`Space` and `ColorValue`, it carries an alternate: `AlternateSpace` and
`AlternateColorValue`, the colour to use where the ink itself is not available. InDesign
writes `AlternateSpace="NoAlternateColor"` with an empty `AlternateColorValue` on process
colours. Library spots, PANTONE colours for example, come with a Lab definition and a Lab
alternate of the same value: `PANTONE 300 C` in a surveyed file has `Model="Spot"`,
`Space="LAB"`, `ColorValue="35.68627450980392 -10 -62"`, `AlternateSpace="LAB"` and the same
numbers again in `AlternateColorValue`.

A spot defined in CMYK carries a CMYK alternate. How a viewer shows a spot colour on
screen is covered on [spot & process color](/docs/idml/color-swatches/spot-and-process).

### `TintValue` on a colour is not a tint

A named tint is written as a `<Tint>` element. A `TintValue` attribute on a `<Color>` is not
how InDesign stores one: InDesign drops the attribute when it opens the file, and the colour
appears at full strength. Write tints as
[`<Tint>`](/docs/idml/color-swatches/gradients-and-tints#named-tints-the-tint-element).

> **In Paged:** CMYK, RGB and Lab colours all render. A spot colour is previewed through its
> CMYK alternate when it has one, and otherwise from its Lab or RGB values. Mixed inks are
> recognised but not resolved to a colour. Paged also honours a `TintValue` on a `<Color>`,
> which InDesign does not. <SupportBadge feature="color-swatches.process-spot-tint" /> <SupportBadge feature="color-swatches.lab-mixed-ink" />

## `<Swatch>`

<AttrTable element="Swatch" />

In InDesign's files the `<Swatch>` element is used for `Swatch/None`, the "no paint" swatch.
A page item with `FillColor="Swatch/None"` has no fill: it is transparent there. The same
spelling turns off a stroke. A `Color` attribute on a `<Swatch>` that names a `<Color>` makes
the swatch an alias for that colour; one surveyed document has one.

## The reserved colours

InDesign writes the same reserved entries into every document's `Graphic.xml`. They are
`<Color>` elements whose `ColorOverride` marks their role:

| `Self` | `ColorOverride` | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `Color/Black` | `Specialblack` | Process black, 100 % K. Not editable. |
| `Color/Paper` | `Specialpaper` | The colour of the paper. A fill of Paper covers what lies beneath it with the paper colour; it is not "no fill". |
| `Color/Registration` | `Specialregistration` | Prints on every plate, for crop and registration marks. `Model="Registration"`. |
| `Color/Cyan`, `Color/Magenta`, `Color/Yellow` | `Hiddenreserved` | The process primaries, hidden from the Swatches panel (`Visible="false"`). |
| user colours | `Normal` | Everything else. |

Black and Paper appear in all 305 surveyed documents, the other reserved entries in the 271
that InDesign wrote; the remaining files come from a generator. A reader should still resolve `Color/Black`,
`Color/Paper` and `Swatch/None` by name when a file leaves them out.

> **In Paged:** the reserved swatches are recognised by their ids, so a file that omits them
> still resolves them. <SupportBadge feature="color-swatches.color-overrides" />

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between `<Color>` and `<Swatch>`?**
A `<Color>` carries a model, a space and channel values. A `<Swatch>` carries no values; in
InDesign's files it is the reserved `Swatch/None`, which means no paint.

**How does IDML spell the Lab colour space?**
`Space="LAB"`, in capitals, with `ColorValue` holding L, a and b, for example
`46.27 20 33`.

**What does `AlternateSpace="NoAlternateColor"` mean?**
That the colour has no alternate. InDesign writes it on process colours, with an empty
`AlternateColorValue`. Spot colours carry a real alternate, in Lab or CMYK.

**Can I make a tint by adding `TintValue` to a `<Color>`?**
No. InDesign ignores that attribute and shows the colour at full strength. A named tint is a
`<Tint>` element with `BaseColor` and `TintValue`.

**Does a document have to define Black, Paper and None?**
InDesign always writes them. A reader should resolve them by name anyway, because files
written by other tools sometimes leave them out.
