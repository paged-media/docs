**A document names the colour profiles its numbers are meant for; it does not contain them. The same CMYK values look different under a different profile.**

**In short:** The `<Document>` element in `designmap.xml` carries the document's working
spaces: `CMYKProfile` and `RGBProfile`, each the name of an ICC profile, plus the rendering
intents `SolidColorIntent`, `AfterBlendingIntent` and `DefaultImageIntent`, and the
`CMYKPolicy` and `RGBPolicy` for files that arrive with other profiles. Only the names travel
with the file. To show CMYK colours accurately, a reader needs the profile itself, found by
that name.

## The profile names

`CMYKProfile` and `RGBProfile` hold a profile's description, the name InDesign shows in its
colour settings. Across the documents surveyed for this reference:

| Attribute | Value | Documents | What it describes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CMYKProfile` | `Coated FOGRA39 (ISO 12647-2:2004)` | most | Offset printing on coated paper, the common European standard |
| `CMYKProfile` | `U.S. Web Coated (SWOP) v2` | many | Web offset printing on coated paper, a common North American standard |
| `CMYKProfile` | `Coated FOGRA27 (ISO 12647-2:2004)` | a few | An older coated-paper offset condition |
| `RGBProfile` | `sRGB IEC61966-2.1` | almost all | The standard screen RGB space |
| `RGBProfile` | `Adobe RGB (1998)` | a few | A wider RGB space used in photography |
| either | `$ID/` | a few | No profile named |

The CMYK profile is the one that matters most. A CMYK `ColorValue` is ink coverage, and what
that coverage looks like depends on the press and paper the profile describes: `65 45 30 10`
is a different blue-grey on coated and on uncoated stock. RGB colours depend on the RGB
profile in the same way, although sRGB is so common that a reader assuming it is rarely far
off. Lab colours need no profile; they describe a colour directly.

## Rendering intents

When a colour has to move from one profile's space into another's, from the CMYK working
space to the screen for example, a rendering intent decides how colours that do not fit are
handled. The document carries three:

- **`SolidColorIntent`** for flat colours: swatches, fills, strokes, text;
- **`DefaultImageIntent`** for placed images (an `<Image>` can override it with its own
  `ImageRenderingIntent`);
- **`AfterBlendingIntent`** for colours that result from transparency.

Every surveyed document has `UseColorSettings` for all three, which defers to the colour
settings of the application that opens the file.

## Profile policies

`CMYKPolicy` and `RGBPolicy` record what InDesign does with content that carries a different
profile. In the surveyed files `CMYKPolicy` is `CombinationOfPreserveAndSafeCmyk` or
`ColorPolicyOff`, and `RGBPolicy` is `PreserveEmbeddedProfiles` or `ColorPolicyOff`. They
govern placing and pasting in InDesign, not how the file's own swatches look.

## Rendering depends on the profile being available

An IDML file contains no ICC profiles. A reader that wants the colours InDesign shows has to
find the named profile on its own system or be given it, and convert CMYK and RGB through it.
A reader without the profile can only approximate; a simple formula from CMYK to RGB is
recognisably off for saturated colours. Two readers that disagree on colour usually disagree
on the profile, not on the numbers.

> **In Paged:** no profile ships with the engine. `paged-inspect` on the command line looks for
> the document's named profile among the Adobe colour profiles installed on the machine. In
> the browser the engine converts colour with qcms (natively with lcms2) and gets full colour
> management once the host registers the profile's bytes under the document's profile name
> and applies its colour settings; until then CMYK is converted by a simple formula. Lab
> colours are converted without a profile. <SupportBadge feature="color-swatches.icc-cmm" />

## Frequently asked questions

**Does an IDML file embed its colour profiles?**
No. `CMYKProfile` and `RGBProfile` on `<Document>` are names. The profiles themselves have to
be available to whoever renders the file.

**What does `Coated FOGRA39 (ISO 12647-2:2004)` mean?**
It is the description of the ICC profile for offset printing on coated paper under
ISO 12647-2, the most common CMYK working space in the surveyed documents.

**Why do CMYK colours look different in different viewers?**
Usually because one of them converts through the named profile and the other does not have
it, or uses another profile.

**What does `UseColorSettings` mean for an intent?**
That the document has no intent of its own and defers to the opening application's colour
settings. It is the value in every surveyed file.

**Do Lab colours need a profile?**
No. Lab describes a colour independently of any device, so it converts to the screen without
one.
