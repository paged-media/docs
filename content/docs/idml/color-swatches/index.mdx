**An IDML document keeps its colours in one palette; every fill and stroke names a palette entry by id.**

**In short:** `Resources/Graphic.xml` holds the palette: colours, named tints, gradients and
the reserved "no paint" swatch. A rectangle, a run of text or a table cell carries no channel
values of its own. It names an entry in `FillColor` or `StrokeColor`, and may scale it with a
tint. Change the entry and everything that names it follows. How the numbers look on screen
also depends on the colour profiles named on the `<Document>` element.

## What the palette holds

`Graphic.xml` is a flat list keyed by `Self` id:

- **`<Color>`**: a colour in CMYK, RGB or Lab, printed as process, as a spot ink, or, for the
  reserved Registration colour, on every plate.
- **`<Tint>`**: a named tint, a colour at a fixed percentage, pointing at its base colour.
- **`<Gradient>`**: a blend whose `<GradientStop>` children point at colours.
- **`<Swatch>`**: in InDesign's files, `Swatch/None`, the absence of paint.

The same part lists the document's inks (`<Ink>`), which output uses. Colour groups, the
folders of the Swatches panel, are kept in `designmap.xml`.

## How a fill resolves

A shape with `FillColor="Color/Red"` is filled with whatever `Color/Red` defines. The id can
name a `<Color>` or a `<Tint>`, which give a flat colour; a `<Gradient>`, which the shape then
places with its own angle and length; or `Swatch/None`, which means no fill. A `FillTint` on the
shape scales the colour for that shape only.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where does an IDML document store its colours?**
In `Resources/Graphic.xml`, as `<Color>`, `<Tint>`, `<Gradient>` and `<Swatch>` elements.
Page items name these by `Self` id; they hold no colour values themselves.

**How does a shape's fill become a colour?**
`FillColor` names a palette entry. A colour or tint gives a flat colour, a gradient is placed
by the shape's gradient attributes, and `Swatch/None` means no fill. `FillTint` can lighten it
for that shape.

**Why can the same file show different colours in different programs?**
CMYK and RGB values are converted to the screen through the colour profiles the document
names. A program without those profiles has to approximate.
