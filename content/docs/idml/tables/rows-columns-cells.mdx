**A table's `<Row>` and `<Column>` children size its tracks; its `<Cell>` children hold the content and most of the formatting.**

**In short:** A `<Column>` carries a width. A `<Row>` carries a height, a minimum and maximum,
and whether it grows with its content (`AutoGrow`) or must stay with the next row
(`KeepWithNextRow`). Both are named by their zero-based index. A `<Cell>` names its grid position
as `Name="column:row"`, can span rows and columns to merge, holds ordinary paragraphs, and
carries its own insets, fill, edge strokes, vertical justification, rotation and diagonal lines.

## Columns

<AttrTable element="Column" attrs={['Name', 'SingleColumnWidth']} />

Columns are listed left to right. `Name` is the column's zero-based index, the first half of a
cell's address, and `SingleColumnWidth` its width in points.

## Rows

<AttrTable element="Row" attrs={['Name', 'SingleRowHeight', 'MinimumHeight', 'AutoGrow']} />

Rows are listed top to bottom across all regions: header rows first, then body, then footer.
`Name` is the row's zero-based index across the whole table. With `AutoGrow` on (the default), a
row is at least `MinimumHeight` tall and grows to fit its tallest cell, up to `MaximumHeight`.
With `AutoGrow="false"` the row stays at its set height whatever its cells hold, and text that
does not fit a cell is overset in that cell.

`KeepWithNextRow="true"` keeps a row in the same frame as the row after it, so a table never breaks
between them. `StartRow` forces a row to begin in the next column, frame or page.

> **In Paged:** row heights, `AutoGrow` and `KeepWithNextRow` are honoured, matched against
> InDesign: a fixed row keeps its height and oversets the lines that do not fit. `StartRow` is not
> read yet. <SupportBadge feature="tables.row-col-sizing" />

## Cells

### Address and span

<AttrTable element="Cell" attrs={['Name', 'RowSpan', 'ColumnSpan', 'AppliedCellStyle']} />

`Name` is the cell's address: column index, a colon, row index, both zero-based, so the cell in
column 1 of row 0 is `Name="1:0"`. InDesign lists cells column by column, but the address, not the
order, places a cell. A cell with `ColumnSpan="2"` covers its own column and the next; a cell with
`RowSpan="3"` covers its row and the two below. The positions it covers have no `<Cell>` of their
own.

> **In Paged:** spans are honoured. A row-spanning cell that needs more height than its rows give
> adds the shortfall to the last row it spans. <SupportBadge feature="tables.spans" />

### Insets and text

A cell's content is ordinary text: one or more `<ParagraphStyleRange>` children, with their own
`<Br/>` marks, exactly as in a story. A cell can even hold a table.

<AttrTable element="Cell" attrs={['TextTopInset', 'TextLeftInset', 'TextBottomInset', 'TextRightInset', 'VerticalJustification', 'RotationAngle', 'MinimumFirstBaselineOffset', 'ClipContentToCell']} />

The four insets pad the text from the cell's edges. `VerticalJustification` places the text
(`TopAlign`, `CenterAlign`, `BottomAlign`, `JustifyAlign`). `RotationAngle` turns the content by
90, 180 or 270 degrees; the borders and fill do not turn. `FirstBaselineOffset` and
`MinimumFirstBaselineOffset` place the first line, as in a text frame. `ClipContentToCell` clips
content that overflows the cell.

> **In Paged:** a cell is composed like a small text frame. Insets come from the cell's own
> attributes, not from its cell style. Vertical justification and rotation are applied. The first
> line is placed by the cell's `FirstBaselineOffset` (the ascent by default), its own or, when it
> states none, its cell style's through `BasedOn`, with `MinimumFirstBaselineOffset` as a floor
> for every value, as in a text frame; each later paragraph sits one leading below the one
> before.

### Fill and edge strokes

<AttrTable element="Cell" attrs={['FillColor', 'FillTint', 'TopEdgeStrokeColor', 'TopEdgeStrokeWeight', 'TopEdgeStrokeTint', 'TopEdgeStrokeType', 'TopEdgeStrokeGapColor']} />

`FillColor` and `FillTint` paint the cell's background. Each edge (`Top…`, `Bottom…`, `Left…`,
`Right…`) has a colour, weight, tint, stroke type and gap colour. When a table style's dividers
apply, InDesign writes the resulting stroke on each cell's matching edge, so these edge attributes
are what most often draws a table's grid. A value on the cell wins over its cell style.

> **In Paged:** the fill colour and the edge strokes are drawn, each edge in its stroke style (a
> double line as two rules, dotted styles as dots, other styles solid). A cell's `FillTint`, else
> its cell style's through `BasedOn`, tints its fill, and a style's edge tints apply the same way.
> Edge gap colours are not painted.
> <SupportBadge feature="tables.borders-strokes" />

### Diagonal lines

`LeftLineDrawn` draws the diagonal from the top-left to the bottom-right corner and
`RightLineDrawn` the one from top-right to bottom-left, each with its own `…StrokeColor`,
`…StrokeWeight` and `…StrokeTint`. `DiagonalLineInFront` puts the lines over the content instead
of behind it.

> **In Paged:** diagonal lines are drawn with their tints and front or back order.
> <SupportBadge feature="tables.cell-diagonals" />

## Frequently asked questions

**How does a `<Cell>` know its grid position?**
From `Name="column:row"`, both zero-based. The order in which cells are listed does not matter.

**How are merged cells written?**
As one `<Cell>` with `RowSpan` or `ColumnSpan` above 1. The grid positions it covers have no cell
element of their own.

**What does `AutoGrow="false"` do?**
It fixes the row at its set height. Text that does not fit a cell in that row is overset in the
cell instead of making the row taller.

**Where does a cell's padding come from?**
From its `TextTopInset`, `TextLeftInset`, `TextBottomInset` and `TextRightInset` attributes, or
from its cell style's insets when the cell sets none.
