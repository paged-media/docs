**An IDML table is not a page item of its own: it is a `<Table>` element that rides on a paragraph in the text flow.**

**In short:** A `<Table>` sits inside a `<CharacterStyleRange>`, inside a
`<ParagraphStyleRange>`, inside a story, where a run of text would sit. It has no position and
names no frame: it draws wherever its paragraph lands as the story flows through its frames.
The table declares its grid with counts of header, body and footer rows and of columns, and
carries `<Row>`, `<Column>` and `<Cell>` children; each cell holds its own paragraphs. The table
takes its paragraph's `<Br/>` mark right after `</Table>` when more text follows.

## The model in one breath

A `<Table>` declares its shape with `HeaderRowCount`, `BodyRowCount`, `FooterRowCount` and
`ColumnCount`, and then lists:

- **`<Row>`** elements, one per row, with the row's height settings;
- **`<Column>`** elements, one per column, with the column's width;
- **`<Cell>`** elements, one per occupied grid position, each addressed by `Name="column:row"`
  and holding its own paragraphs of text.

A cell can span several rows or columns, so a table usually has fewer `<Cell>` elements than
rows times columns.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is a table represented in IDML?**
As a `<Table>` element inside a `<CharacterStyleRange>` of a story. The table declares its grid
with row and column counts and lists `<Row>`, `<Column>` and `<Cell>` children.

**Where does an IDML table appear on the page?**
Where its paragraph lands as the story flows through its frames. A table has no position of its
own; move the paragraph and the table moves with it.

**Why are there fewer `<Cell>` elements than rows times columns?**
Because merged cells cover several grid positions with one `<Cell>`. The positions a merged cell
covers have no element of their own.

**Can a table contain another table?**
Yes. A cell holds ordinary paragraphs, and one of them can hold a `<Table>`.
