**Page items are the children of a spread after its pages; layers set the order between layers, and document order sets it within a layer.**

**In short:** After its `<Page>` elements, a `<Spread>` lists its page items: `TextFrame`,
`Rectangle`, `Oval`, `GraphicLine`, `Polygon` and `Group` (and rarer kinds such as
`EPSText`). Items can nest: a group holds members, and a frame can hold content pasted into
it. There is no z-index attribute. Within one layer, the item written first is painted
first and sits at the back. Across layers, the layer order in the design map wins: it lists
layers from bottom to top.

## What a spread contains

- **Shapes and frames** as direct children of the `<Spread>`. See
  [page-item types](/docs/idml/frames-paths/page-item-types).
- **Groups**: a `<Group>` holds its members as child elements, in their own back-to-front
  order. See [groups](/docs/idml/frames-paths/groups).
- **Pasted-in content**: a `Rectangle`, `Oval` or `Polygon` can hold another page item as a
  child element (InDesign's *Paste Into*, and every placed image). The child's
  `ItemTransform` is relative to the host, and the host's path clips it.
- **Anchored objects** are not here: they live in the story, inside the text they are
  anchored to. See [anchored objects](/docs/idml/anchored-inline-objects/anchored-objects).

## Document order within a layer

Items on the same layer paint in the order they are written: the first one is at the back,
the last one at the front. A group's members paint at the group's position in that order,
in their own order, as one unit. A pasted-in child paints with its host, inside the host's
outline.

## Layer order across layers

Every page item can name a layer with `ItemLayer`. The `<Layer>` elements in
`designmap.xml` are listed **from bottom to top**: everything on the first-listed layer
paints before everything on the second, whatever the order in the spread.

  above the blue rectangle on the first-listed layer.
</ExampleEmbed>

Two rules about groups and layers:

- A group's members are on the **group's** layer. InDesign keeps a group on one layer and
  ignores an `ItemLayer` on a member that disagrees.
- A `<Group>` with no `ItemLayer` lands on the document's first layer (the bottom one) and
  takes its members with it. A writer should always give a group the layer its members
  should be on.

Layers in full are covered under [Layers](/docs/idml/layers).

## Frequently asked questions

**How is the stacking order of page items determined?**
By layer first, then by document order. Layers paint from the first `<Layer>` in the design
map (bottom) to the last (top); within a layer, the item written first is at the back.

**Is there a z-index attribute?**
No. Position in the XML, together with the layer, is the stacking order. To bring an item
forward, move its element later in the spread or onto a higher layer.

**Which layer are a group's members on?**
The group's. InDesign keeps members on their group's layer; a group without `ItemLayer`
goes to the document's first layer.

**What is pasted-in content?**
A page item written as a child of a `Rectangle`, `Oval` or `Polygon`. Its transform is
relative to the host frame and the host's outline clips it. A placed image is the most
common case.
