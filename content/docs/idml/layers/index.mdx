**A layer in IDML is a named, document-wide plane that page items belong to; it decides stacking between layers and whether its items show.**

**In short:** Layers are declared once, in `designmap.xml`, as `<Layer>` elements listed from
the **bottom** layer to the top. A page item joins a layer by naming it in `ItemLayer`; the
layer holds no list of members. A layer says nothing about position. It does three things a
reader must honour: its place in the list orders its items against other layers' items, and
its `Visible` and `Printable` switches decide whether its items appear. The rest is for
editing: `Locked`, guides, the panel colour.

## Two halves in two parts of the package

- **Definitions** live in `designmap.xml`: one `<Layer>` per layer, with an id, a name and its
  switches, in bottom-to-top order.
- **Membership** lives on the page items: each item's `ItemLayer` names its layer by `Self`
  id. A group's members are on the group's layer.

Items can also be hidden one at a time: `Visible="false"` on a page item hides that item
whatever its layer says.

The day an item you can see in the XML does not appear on the page, check here first: follow
its `ItemLayer` (or its group's) to the `<Layer>` in the design map and read `Visible` and
`Printable`, then check the item's own `Visible`.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a layer in an IDML document?**
A named, document-wide plane that page items are assigned to. It orders its items against
other layers and can hide them, lock them or keep them from printing. It holds no position.

**Where are layers defined?**
In `designmap.xml`, as `<Layer>` elements. The first one listed is the bottom layer. Items join
a layer through their `ItemLayer` attribute.

**Why would an item in the XML not appear on the page?**
Usually a hidden or non-printing layer, or `Visible="false"` on the item itself. Follow its
`ItemLayer` to the design map, or check the item's own `Visible`.
