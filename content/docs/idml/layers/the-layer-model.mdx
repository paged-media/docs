**A layer is defined once in the design map; a page item joins it by naming its id in `ItemLayer`, and the design map's order is the layers' stacking order, bottom first.**

**In short:** Layers are `<Layer>` children of the `Document` element in `designmap.xml`. Each
has a `Self` id, a `Name` and switches: `Visible`, `Printable`, `Locked`, and a few for guides
and the panel. They are listed **from bottom to top**: items on the first layer paint first,
under everything on later layers. A page item names its layer in `ItemLayer`. Group members
are on the group's layer, and a group without `ItemLayer` lands on the first layer.

## The `<Layer>` element

<AttrTable element="Layer" />

- **`Self`** is what `ItemLayer` refers to.
- **`Name`** is the label in InDesign's Layers panel.
- **`Visible`**, **`Printable`** and **`Locked`** are covered on
  [visibility and printing](/docs/idml/layers/visibility-and-printing).
- **`ShowGuides`**, **`LockGuides`**, **`IgnoreWrap`** (items on the layer cause no text wrap),
  **`Expendable`** and **`UI`**, and the panel colour **`LayerColor`**, do not change what a
  page looks like apart from `IgnoreWrap`.

## The order of the list is the stacking order

The design map lists layers from the bottom of the Layers panel to the top. Everything on the
first-listed layer paints before everything on the second, and so on; within one layer, the
order of the items in the spread decides. So a background layer comes first in the design map
and a text layer last. Real documents read that way: one lists `Background`, `Placeholders`,
`Design Elements`, `Text`.

## `ItemLayer`

A layer holds no list of members. Each page item names its layer:

<AttrTable element="Rectangle" attrs={['ItemLayer']} />

`ItemLayer` appears on `TextFrame`, `Rectangle`, `Oval`, `GraphicLine`, `Polygon` and
`Group`, and on ruler guides.

### Groups put their members on their own layer

A group and its members are on one layer, the group's. A member's own `ItemLayer` does not
take it out of the group's layer. A `<Group>` written **without** `ItemLayer` lands on the
document's first layer, the bottom one, and takes its members with it, whatever layers the
members name. Write `ItemLayer` on every group.

### A missing or unknown layer

InDesign writes `ItemLayer` on page items in documents with layers. For a group, leaving it
out means the first layer (above). A reader should treat any other item without a resolvable
`ItemLayer` as visible: dropping content because a reference is missing is worse than drawing
it.

## Nested layers

A `<Layer>` element written inside another `<Layer>` is a child layer: hiding or locking the
parent hides or locks the children. None of the documents surveyed for this reference nests
layers; InDesign's own files list them flat.

> **In Paged:** layers are read in design-map order and sort items bottom to top; nested
> `<Layer>` elements are read as a hierarchy, a child inheriting a hidden or locked parent.
> An item whose `ItemLayer` is missing or unknown is drawn.

## Frequently asked questions

**How does a page item get onto a layer?**
Its `ItemLayer` attribute names the layer's `Self` id. The reference runs from item to layer;
the layer lists no members.

**Which layer is on top?**
The last one listed in `designmap.xml`. The list runs from the bottom layer to the top layer,
so the first `<Layer>` paints first.

**Which layer are group members on?**
The group's. A group without `ItemLayer` goes to the first, bottom layer, members included.

**What happens if ItemLayer names a layer that does not exist?**
Nothing defines what that layer would hide, so a reader should draw the item as if it were on
a visible layer.
