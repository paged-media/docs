**IDML has six page-item elements; they share one skeleton and differ in a few fields each.**

**In short:** `TextFrame`, `Rectangle`, `Oval`, `GraphicLine`, `Polygon` and `Group` all
carry a `Self` id, an `ItemTransform`, an outline in `<Properties><PathGeometry>`, paint
attributes, an object style, a layer, and `Visible`, `Locked` and `Name`. What sets them
apart: a text frame names a story, a rectangle can shape its corners, an oval is an ellipse
by its path, a line can carry arrowheads, a polygon can hold several contours, and a group
holds other items. This page is the reference for both parts.

## Attributes every page item shares

<AttrTable element="Rectangle" attrs={['Self', 'ItemTransform', 'ItemLayer', 'Visible', 'Locked', 'Name', 'AppliedObjectStyle', 'FillColor', 'FillTint', 'StrokeColor', 'StrokeWeight', 'StrokeType', 'StrokeTint', 'GradientFillAngle', 'GradientFillLength', 'GradientFillStart']} />

- **`FillColor` / `StrokeColor`** name a swatch in `Resources/Graphic.xml` (`Color/…`,
  `Tint/…`, `Gradient/…`), or `Swatch/None` for none.
- **`FillTint` / `StrokeTint`** are percentages. `-1` is InDesign's *unset* value: the swatch's
  own tint applies.
- **`StrokeType`** names a stroke style: the built-in solid, dashed, dotted, striped and wavy
  styles, or a custom style defined in the document.
- **`GradientFillAngle`, `GradientFillLength`, `GradientFillStart`** place a gradient fill on
  the item: its direction, its length and its start point in the item's space.
- **`Visible="false"`** hides this item alone. **`Locked="true"`** only blocks editing.
  **`Name`** is the item's name in the Layers panel; `$ID/` means unnamed.
- Effects (drop shadows, glows, feathers, opacity and blend modes) are written as
  `<TransparencySetting>`, `<StrokeTransparencySetting>` and related child elements.

> **In Paged:** all of these are read and honoured, including dashed, dotted, striped and wavy
> strokes and gap colours. <SupportBadge feature="frames-paths.stroke-striped-wavy" />

## TextFrame

The only member with a `ParentStory`: the story whose text it holds. `PreviousTextFrame`
and `NextTextFrame` thread it to other frames (`n` for none). Its `TextFramePreference`
child (columns, insets, vertical justification, first baseline, auto-sizing) has its own
page: [text frames](/docs/idml/frames-paths/text-frames).

<AttrTable element="TextFrame" attrs={['ParentStory', 'PreviousTextFrame', 'NextTextFrame', 'ContentType']} />

## Rectangle

A panel when filled, a picture frame when it holds an `<Image>`, `<EPS>` or `<PDF>`, an
outline otherwise. Its path has four corner anchors.

Corners can be shaped without changing the path. `CornerOption` and `CornerRadius` apply to
all four corners; `TopLeftCornerOption` and `TopLeftCornerRadius` (and their siblings) set
one corner. The values InDesign writes are `None`, `RoundedCorner`, `InverseRoundedCorner`,
`InsetCorner`, `BevelCorner` and `FancyCorner`.

`StrokeAlignment` places the stroke centred on the path, inside it or outside it
(`CenterAlignment`, `InsideAlignment`, `OutsideAlignment`). `EndJoin` and `MiterLimit` shape
the corners of the stroke.

<AttrTable element="Rectangle" attrs={['CornerOption', 'CornerRadius', 'TopLeftCornerOption', 'TopLeftCornerRadius', 'StrokeAlignment', 'EndJoin', 'MiterLimit', 'ContentType']} />

> **In Paged:** all five corner treatments draw their own geometry, per corner.
> <SupportBadge feature="frames-paths.corner-options" /> Inside and outside stroke alignment is
> exact on rectangles; on polygons and open paths the stroke stays centred.
> <SupportBadge feature="frames-paths.stroke-alignment" />

## Oval

An ellipse, because its path is one: four anchors at the midpoints of the edges (bottom,
right, top, left) with handles along each edge. An `<Oval>` with a four-corner path is drawn
by InDesign as a rectangle; the element name does not make the curve. See
[how an oval is spelled](/docs/idml/geometry-coordinates/path-geometry).

<AttrTable element="Oval" attrs={['ItemTransform', 'FillColor', 'StrokeColor', 'StrokeWeight', 'ContentType']} />

## GraphicLine

A rule: an open path of two anchors, which may be curved. Its ends are its first and last
anchors, in the order they are written; nothing is derived from a bounding box. It has no
fill region worth painting.

Line ends are set with `LeftLineEnd` (at the first anchor) and `RightLineEnd` (at the last),
scaled by `LeftArrowHeadScale` and `RightArrowHeadScale` (percent, default 100). The values are
`None`, `SimpleArrowHead`, `SimpleWideArrowHead`, `TriangleArrowHead`,
`TriangleWideArrowHead`, `BarbedArrowHead`, `CurvedArrowHead`, `CircleArrowHead`,
`CircleSolidArrowHead`, `SquareArrowHead`, `SquareSolidArrowHead` and `BarArrowHead`. An open
`Polygon` (a pen or pencil path) can carry them too. `EndCap` shapes the ends without
arrowheads.

<AttrTable element="GraphicLine" attrs={['StrokeColor', 'StrokeWeight', 'StrokeType', 'EndCap', 'LeftLineEnd', 'RightLineEnd']} />

> **In Paged:** arrowheads are drawn at both ends of lines and open polygons, scaled by the
> arrowhead scale. <SupportBadge feature="frames-paths.arrowheads" />

## Polygon

Any other outline: stars, charts, curves, pen paths. Several `GeometryPathType` contours make
a [compound path](/docs/idml/geometry-coordinates/compound-paths), and an open contour makes
a path that is only stroked.

<AttrTable element="Polygon" limit={8} />

## Group

A container. Its members are child elements, in their own back-to-front order; each member's
`ItemTransform` is relative to the group, and the group's own transform moves them all. A
group can carry its own transparency (opacity, blend mode, drop shadow) applied to the
members as a whole, and its `ItemLayer` decides the layer of all its members. See
[groups](/docs/idml/frames-paths/groups).

## Frequently asked questions

**Which attributes do all page items share?**
`Self`, `ItemTransform`, the `PathGeometry` outline, the paint attributes (`FillColor`,
`FillTint`, `StrokeColor`, `StrokeWeight`, `StrokeType`, `StrokeTint`), `AppliedObjectStyle`,
`ItemLayer`, `Visible`, `Locked` and `Name`.

**What is the difference between a Rectangle and an Oval?**
Their paths. A rectangle's path has four corner anchors; an oval's path has four mid-edge
anchors with curve handles. InDesign draws each from its path, so an `<Oval>` written with
corner anchors comes out as a rectangle.

**What does FillTint="-1" mean?**
It is InDesign's unset value: no tint of the item's own, so the swatch applies as defined.

**Are corner options and arrowheads part of the path?**
No. Corner options are attributes applied on top of the square corners of the path, and
arrowheads are drawn at the path's end anchors. The path stays as written.

**How are the ends of a GraphicLine determined?**
By its first and last path anchors. `LeftLineEnd` belongs to the first anchor, `RightLineEnd`
to the last.
