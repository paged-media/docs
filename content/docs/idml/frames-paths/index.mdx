**Everything visible on an IDML page is a page item: one of a small family of elements that share one skeleton.**

**In short:** A page item is the element that puts something on a page: body copy, a photo,
a tinted panel, a rule, a logo. The family is small: `TextFrame`, `Rectangle`, `Oval`,
`GraphicLine`, `Polygon` and `Group`. Page items are children of a
[`Spread`](/docs/idml/layout-model/spreads-and-pages), of a `Group`, or of a frame they were
pasted into. Each one has a `Self` id, an `ItemTransform` that places it in its parent, a
`PathGeometry` outline, and paint and style attributes. Learn the skeleton once and every
member reads the same way.

## One family, two jobs

- **Frames that hold text.** A `TextFrame` names a story in `ParentStory`, and the story's
  text flows into the frame's outline.
- **Frames that hold graphics, or nothing.** A `Rectangle`, `Oval`, `GraphicLine` or
  `Polygon` is a vector shape. With a fill it is a panel; with a placed `<Image>` (or `EPS`,
  `PDF`) inside it is a picture frame; with neither it is an outline.

The element name tells the current role, and `ContentType` (`TextType`, `GraphicType`,
`Unassigned`) says the same thing as an attribute.

## The members

| Element | What it is |
|---|---|
| `TextFrame` | A frame bound to a story by `ParentStory`. |
| `Rectangle` | The workhorse: panels, picture frames, empty boxes. |
| `Oval` | An ellipse, drawn from its four-anchor path. |
| `GraphicLine` | A two-point open path: a rule, with optional line ends. |
| `Polygon` | Any other outline: stars, curves, compound shapes, pen paths. |
| `Group` | A container whose members move, and can be made transparent, as one. |

## The shared skeleton

Strip any member down and the same parts remain:

- a **`Self`** id, by which threading, groups and overrides refer to it;
- an **[`ItemTransform`](/docs/idml/geometry-coordinates/item-transform)** that places it in its
  parent: the spread, a group, or a host frame;
- a **[`PathGeometry`](/docs/idml/geometry-coordinates/path-geometry)** in `<Properties>` that
  gives its outline, in its own space;
- **paint**: `FillColor`, `FillTint`, `StrokeColor`, `StrokeWeight`, `StrokeType` and more;
- an **[`AppliedObjectStyle`](/docs/idml/styles/object-styles)** that supplies whatever the item
  leaves unset;
- **`ItemLayer`**, the [layer](/docs/idml/layers) it is on;
- **`Visible`**, **`Locked`** and **`Name`**: an item can be hidden on its own, independently of
  its layer; locked against editing; and given a name (`$ID/` means none). Scripts and plugins
  can attach key/value metadata as `<Properties><Label>`.

## Frames inside frames

A `Rectangle`, `Oval` or `Polygon` can hold another page item as a child element. InDesign
writes this for *Paste Into*, and every placed image is the same shape: an `<Image>` inside
its frame. The child's `ItemTransform` is relative to the host, and the host's outline clips
the child. <SupportBadge feature="frames-paths.nested-content" />

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a page item in IDML?**
Any element that puts content on a page: `TextFrame`, `Rectangle`, `Oval`, `GraphicLine`,
`Polygon` or `Group`. Each carries its own placement, outline and paint.

**What is the difference between a text frame and a graphic frame?**
A `TextFrame` names a story in `ParentStory` and holds its text. A graphic frame is a shape
that is filled, holds a placed image, or is empty.

**Can a page item be hidden without hiding its layer?**
Yes. `Visible="false"` on the item hides that item alone. A hidden layer hides every item on
it.

**Where do page items live in the package?**
In `Spreads/Spread_*.xml` and `MasterSpreads/MasterSpread_*.xml`, as children of the spread,
of a group, or of a frame. Anchored objects live in the story instead.
