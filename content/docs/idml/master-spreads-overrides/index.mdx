**A master spread is layout drawn once and shown on many pages; an override is how one page replaces or removes a single master item.**

**In short:** A long document repeats itself: the same running head, the same folio, the
same background on page after page. In IDML that furniture lives once, on a master spread,
and each body page that names the master in `AppliedMaster` shows the items of the master
page at its position, behind its own items. An **override** is the exception: a body page
lists master items in its `OverrideList`, and those items no longer come from the master;
the page usually holds its own edited copy instead. This chapter takes the two halves in
turn: what a master contributes, and what an override takes away.

## Why a reader ends up here

A page shows too much, too little, or the wrong thing from its master. Predicting a body
page means holding two facts together:

- **What the master contributes.** Master items are not written into the body spread.
  Which ones reach a page, and where, follows from `AppliedMaster`, the position of the page
  in its spread, `MasterPageTransform` and the spread's `ShowMasterItems`.
- **What the page takes away.** The page's `OverrideList` names master items it has taken
  over. Those are not shown from the master; the page's own copy, if it has one, is an
  ordinary page item.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a master spread in IDML?**
A spread in a `MasterSpreads/` part whose items appear on every body page that names it in
`AppliedMaster`. Its structure is the same as a body spread's.

**What is the difference between stamping and override resolution?**
Stamping is what the master adds to a page: the items of the matching master page, placed
on the body page behind its own items. Override resolution is what the page takes away: the
master items named in its `OverrideList` are left out.

**Why does a master item not appear in the body page's XML?**
Because it is inherited, not copied. Only an overridden item has a copy in the body spread,
and that copy is then an ordinary page item of the body page.
