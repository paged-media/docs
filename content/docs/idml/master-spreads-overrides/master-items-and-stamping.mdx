**A body page shows the items of the master page in its own position, mapped onto it by `MasterPageTransform`, behind its own items.**

**In short:** A master item is never written into a body spread. It stays in the
`MasterSpreads/` part, and every body page that applies the master shows it. Four things
decide what a page shows: `AppliedMaster` picks the master; the page's position in its
spread picks the master page (first from first, second from second); `MasterPageTransform`
maps that master page onto the body page; and the body spread's `ShowMasterItems` can hide
the whole master layer. Master items keep their master's stacking order and sit behind the
page's own items. Placing master items on a page this way is called *stamping* here.

## Which master page feeds which body page

A master spread has pages of its own, placed in its spread space exactly as body pages are.
A master item belongs to the master page its geometry falls on. A body page takes the items
of the master page at its own position in its spread: on a facing spread, the left body page
takes the master's left page and the right body page its right page. A one-page master
feeds every body page that applies it.

So a master can hold a left-page folio at the outer left corner and a right-page folio at the
outer right corner, and each body page gets only its own.

Neither rule is in the body spread's XML: both come from the master part.

## `MasterPageTransform`

`MasterPageTransform` on a body `<Page>` maps the master page onto that page. It is six
numbers, like `ItemTransform`, and is almost always the identity: the master page's
top-left corner lands on the body page's top-left corner. A non-identity value shifts,
scales or rotates the master layer on that one page.

## `ShowMasterItems`

`ShowMasterItems="false"` on the **body** `<Spread>` hides the master items on all its pages.
The flag belongs to the spread: InDesign ignores it on a `<Page>`. The same attribute on the
`<MasterSpread>` does not hide anything on the body pages that apply the master; the
[two-page-spread](/docs/idml/layout-model/spreads-and-pages) example carries
`ShowMasterItems="false"` on its master and InDesign shows the master's rules on both body
pages.

## Stacking

Master items keep their order from the master spread: the item written first is at the back.
Groups on a master are shown with their members. As a whole they paint behind the body
page's own items, so a background panel on the master never covers the page's text.

InDesign's own rule adds layers: a master item paints behind page items **on the same
layer**, and still in layer order against items on other layers, so a master item on the top
layer appears above page items on a lower layer.

## Text on masters

A master text frame's story is laid out separately on every page that shows it. A folio
holding a page-number marker therefore shows each body page's own number, and a running head
holding a section marker or a running-head variable shows the value for that page. See
[text variables](/docs/idml/sections-numbering-variables/text-variables).

## The `MasterSpread` element

<AttrTable element="MasterSpread" />

`Name` is the composed display name (`A-Master`), made of `NamePrefix` (`A`) and `BaseName`
(`Master`). `PrimaryTextFrame` names a master text frame that receives the text when pages
are added automatically. `OverriddenPageItemProps` is editing bookkeeping and does not
change the result.

> **In Paged:** the master page is chosen by position, items are routed by the centre of their
> bounds (a large item that covers half a master page or more also reaches a page it overlaps),
> `MasterPageTransform` and the master spread's rotation are applied as full matrices, and
> `ShowMasterItems` on the body spread is honoured. Master items paint in the master's
> document order with groups expanded to their members, behind *all* of the page's own items
> regardless of layer. Group transparency and drop shadows on master items are not applied,
> and a master based on another master contributes only its own items.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why don't master items appear in the body page's XML?**
Because pages inherit them. They stay in the `MasterSpreads/` part; only an overridden item
gets a copy in the body spread.

**Which master page does a body page use?**
The one at the same position: the first page of a body spread takes the master's first page,
the second takes the second. A single-page master feeds every page.

**Does ShowMasterItems on the master spread hide its items?**
No. The body spread's `ShowMasterItems` decides whether its pages show master items.
InDesign ignores the attribute on `<Page>` and the master's own value does not hide items on
body pages.

**Do master items render behind the page's own content?**
Behind the page's items on the same layer. Across layers, the layer order still applies in
InDesign.

**Are grouped master items shown?**
Yes. A group on a master reaches the body pages like any other master item.
