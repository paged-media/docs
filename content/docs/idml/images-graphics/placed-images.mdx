**A placed image is a frame with a graphic element inside it: `<Image>` for pixels, `<PDF>` for a PDF page, `<EPS>` for PostScript. The graphic carries its own transform and a `<Link>` to its file.**

**In short:** The frame is an ordinary `Rectangle`, `Oval` or `Polygon` whose outline crops
the picture. Inside it, the graphic element has an `ItemTransform` that places the picture in
the frame, a `GraphicBounds` box in the picture's own units, and a `<Link>` whose
`LinkResourceURI` names the file. An embedded picture keeps its bytes in the graphic's
`<Properties><Contents>`, and its link says `StoredState="Embedded"`. The frame's
`<FrameFittingOption>` records how the picture was fitted and cropped.

## The shape of a graphic frame

<ExampleEmbed example="placed-image">
  A rectangle holding an Image at half scale. The Image's GraphicBounds is the picture's
  600 x 400 pixel box; its Link names the file, which this package does not include.
</ExampleEmbed>

Nothing on the frame's own element says "graphic frame": the child graphic does. A frame with
no graphic child is an empty frame, or a coloured shape.

In the surveyed documents, `<Image>` sits in rectangles, ovals and polygons; `<PDF>` and `<EPS>`
in rectangles.

## Two transforms

- The **frame's** `ItemTransform` places the frame in its spread or group, as for any page item.
- The **graphic's** `ItemTransform` places the picture inside the frame: it maps the picture's
  own space into the frame's inner space. A scale of `0.5` shows the picture at half size; a
  translation moves it within the frame.

The picture lands where the two composed put it, and only the part inside the frame's outline
shows. Cropping is therefore not a property of the picture: it is the frame's outline cutting
off what the graphic's transform puts outside it.

## `<Image>`

<AttrTable element="Image" />

- **`ItemTransform`** places the picture in the frame (above).
- **`GraphicBounds`**, a typed `<Properties>` child with `Left`, `Top`, `Right` and `Bottom`,
  is the picture's own box: for a raster image, `0 0` to its width and height in pixels, one
  pixel to one unit. The transform maps that box into the frame. InDesign reads it that way:
  a `GraphicBounds` set to the frame's box under a scaling transform puts a tiny picture in
  the wrong place.
- **`Space`** (`$ID/RGB`, `$ID/CMYK`, …), **`ActualPpi`** and **`EffectivePpi`** describe the
  picture as placed: its colour space, its own resolution and its resolution at the placed
  size, written as `(x y)` pairs. They are informational.
- **`ImageRenderingIntent`** and a `<Profile>` property record the colour handling of the
  picture; see [colour management](/docs/idml/color-swatches/colour-management).

`<Image>` can also carry `ClippingPathSettings`, `TextWrapPreference` and a
`TransparencySetting` of its own. Clipping paths are covered on
[formats & clipping](/docs/idml/images-graphics/formats-and-clipping).

## `<PDF>` and `<EPS>`

A placed PDF page is a `<PDF>` element, a placed EPS file an `<EPS>` element; there is no
`<EPSImage>`. Both sit in the frame where an `<Image>` would, with the same `ItemTransform`,
`GraphicBounds` and `<Link>`. `<PDF>` adds a `<PDFAttribute>` child with the `PageNumber`
placed, the `PDFCrop` box and `TransparentBackground`, and a `<GraphicLayerOption>` for the
PDF's layers. Placed InDesign pages use `<ImportedPage>`; none of the surveyed documents has
one.

## `<Link>`

<AttrTable element="Link" />

- **`LinkResourceURI`** names the file. InDesign writes an absolute `file:` URI with a single
  slash and percent-encoded spaces: `file:/Users/name/Links/photo.psd` on a Mac,
  `file:C:/Projects/Links/Logo%20grey.pdf` on Windows. A relative URI resolves to nothing in
  InDesign: the frame keeps its geometry and shows a missing link.
- **`StoredState`** is `Normal` for a linked file and `Embedded` when the picture's bytes are
  inside the document. The surveyed files hold both in roughly equal numbers.
- **`LinkResourceFormat`** names the file type, in InDesign's own words: `$ID/JPEG`,
  `$ID/Photoshop`, `$ID/Portable Network Graphics (PNG)`,
  `$ID/Adobe Portable Document Format (PDF)`, `$ID/WEBP ` (with a trailing space).
- The rest (`LinkResourceSize`, `LinkImportTime`, `LinkClassID`, `CanEmbed`, …) is link
  management for InDesign's Links panel.

## Embedded pictures

An embedded picture keeps its bytes in the graphic element as a typed `<Properties>` child,
`<Contents>`, holding the file base64-encoded. The `<Link>` stays, naming where the file
originally came from, with `StoredState="Embedded"`. A reader should prefer the embedded bytes
when they are present. For an `<EPS>`, the contents are PostScript, not pixels.

## Frame fitting

<AttrTable element="FrameFittingOption" />

`<FrameFittingOption>` on the frame records the fitting the user chose and the crop amounts:
`LeftCrop`, `TopCrop`, `RightCrop` and `BottomCrop` in points, negative where the picture
extends past the frame; `FittingOnEmptyFrame` for a picture placed into the frame later
(`FillProportionally`, `Proportionally`, `FitContentToFrame` or `None`);
`FittingAlignment`, the reference point; and `AutoFit`, which re-fits the picture when the
frame is resized. Where the picture sits now is given by the graphic's `ItemTransform`; the
fitting option is how that position came about.

> **In Paged:** the graphic's transform, its link and its embedded `<Contents>` are read; the
> embedded bytes win over the link. Linked files are found at render time by the host. A
> frame whose graphic has no transform is filled by the picture, less the
> `FrameFittingOption` crops. The `ActualPpi`, `EffectivePpi` and `Space` metadata are read
> for display in the editor. <SupportBadge feature="images-graphics.placed-images" /> <SupportBadge feature="images-graphics.frame-fitting" />

## Frequently asked questions

**How does an IDML frame point at its image file?**
Through the `LinkResourceURI` of the `<Link>` inside the graphic element. InDesign writes an
absolute `file:` URI.

**What is `GraphicBounds` on an `<Image>`?**
The picture's own box: for a raster image, zero to its pixel width and height. The image's
`ItemTransform` maps that box into the frame.

**How can I tell an embedded image from a linked one?**
The `<Link>` says `StoredState="Embedded"`, and the graphic element carries the bytes as
base64 in `<Properties><Contents>`.

**Is it `<EPS>` or `<EPSImage>`?**
`<EPS>`. There is no `<EPSImage>` element.

**Why does InDesign show a missing link for my generated file?**
Usually because the URI is relative. InDesign resolves only absolute `file:` URIs; write the
full path, or embed the picture.
