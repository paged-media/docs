**A picture in an IDML layout is always placed inside a frame.**

**In short:** A graphic in InDesign lives in a frame, the same `Rectangle`, `Oval` or
`Polygon` that could hold a colour. The frame owns the geometry: where the picture appears
and what outline crops it. Inside the frame, a graphic element (`<Image>`, `<PDF>` or
`<EPS>`) adds three things: a transform that places the picture in the frame, the picture's
own bounds, and a `<Link>` to the file. Most pictures are linked rather than embedded, so the
pixels are usually not in the package at all.

## Frame and picture are separate

Moving a frame moves the picture with it; moving the picture inside the frame changes the
crop. In IDML the two are separate elements with separate transforms: the frame's
`ItemTransform` places the frame on the spread, and the graphic's `ItemTransform` places the
picture inside the frame. Read [frames & paths](/docs/idml/frames-paths) and
[geometry & coordinates](/docs/idml/geometry-coordinates) first, and a graphic frame holds no
surprises: it is a frame with one more child.

The frame also remembers how the picture was fitted, in `<FrameFittingOption>`: fill the frame
proportionally, fit the content, crop amounts. The picture's actual position is always the
graphic's transform.

## The pixels live elsewhere

The graphic's `<Link>` names the picture's file with an absolute `file:` URI. Unless the
picture is embedded, the IDML package does not contain it, and anyone rendering the document
needs the linked files as well. InDesign packages a document with a `Links` folder for that
reason. A document whose links are missing still opens: every frame keeps its geometry, and
only the pictures are absent.

The graphic element also carries facts about the placed picture, written by InDesign for its
Links panel: its colour space (`Space`), its own resolution (`ActualPpi`) and its resolution
at the placed size (`EffectivePpi`).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a placed image in IDML?**
A frame (`Rectangle`, `Oval` or `Polygon`) with a graphic child, usually `<Image>`. The frame
crops; the graphic's transform places the picture; its `<Link>` names the file.

**Are images stored inside the IDML package?**
Only embedded ones, as base64 in the graphic's `<Contents>`. Linked images are files outside
the package, named by URI.

**What happens when a linked image is missing?**
The document still opens and every frame keeps its geometry; only the picture is missing.
