**`GeometricBounds` is an element's axis-aligned bounding box, written in the y-before-x order `y1 x1 y2 x2`.**

**In short:** `GeometricBounds` is four numbers in points: **top, left, bottom, right**,
with the vertical coordinate first. Width is `x2 − x1` and height is `y2 − y1`. Every
`<Page>` carries it, and it is the page's only geometry. On page items it is optional and
rare: InDesign's own exports describe a page item by its
[`PathGeometry`](/docs/idml/geometry-coordinates/path-geometry), and a reader derives the
box from the path. The bounds are in the element's own space; its
[`ItemTransform`](/docs/idml/geometry-coordinates/item-transform) maps them into the parent.

## The axis order is y before x

```text
GeometricBounds="y1 x1 y2 x2"      top  left  bottom  right
```

<AttrTable
  element="GeometricBounds (y1 x1 y2 x2)"

    { attr: 'y2', type: 'number (pt)', note: 'Bottom edge: the larger Y, because Y grows downward.' },
    { attr: 'x2', type: 'number (pt)', note: 'Right edge.' },
  ]}
/>

A value with any count other than four numbers is not a box. A page declared
`GeometricBounds="0 0 841.89 595.276"` is 595.276 pt wide and 841.89 pt tall: an A4
page whose own space starts at its top-left corner. Page bounds do not have to start at
`0 0`; what places the page is the bounds mapped through its `ItemTransform`.

## Bounds and the outline

`GeometricBounds` is only a box. For an axis-aligned rectangle it matches the outline; for
an ellipse, a triangle or a curve it is the box around the outline. Where it is absent, the
box comes from the path: the smallest and largest x and y over the path's anchor points.

Most page items in real documents carry no `GeometricBounds` at all: InDesign writes the
path and nothing else. A writer that emits both should keep them consistent.

## Where it appears

| Element | Use |
|---|---|
| `Page` | The page rectangle in the page's own space. Required: a page without it has no size. |
| page items | Optional. Derived from the `PathGeometry` when absent. |

The bounds are in the element's own inner space, the same space as its path anchors.
The element's `ItemTransform` maps that space into the parent: the spread for a page or a
top-level item, a group for a group member.

> **In Paged:** a page without parseable `GeometricBounds` is skipped. On a page item that
> carries both, the attribute wins over the path. <SupportBadge feature="geometry-coordinates.geometric-bounds" />

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the axis order of GeometricBounds?**
`y1 x1 y2 x2`: top, left, bottom, right. The vertical coordinate comes first, unlike path
anchors, which are written `x y`.

**How are width and height computed?**
Width is `x2 − x1`, height is `y2 − y1`. Because Y grows downward, `y2` is the larger
value.

**Why do my page items have no GeometricBounds?**
Because InDesign does not write them on page items. It writes the outline as
`PathGeometry`; the bounding box is the extent of the path's anchors.

**Are GeometricBounds in page coordinates?**
No. They are in the element's own space. Map them through the element's `ItemTransform`,
and through any enclosing group's, to get spread coordinates.
