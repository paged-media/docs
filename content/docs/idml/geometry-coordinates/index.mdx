**IDML places every page item in its spread's coordinate space, measured in points with Y growing downward, through a chain of affine transforms.**

**In short:** Distances are points (1/72 inch). Y grows downward. A page item is not
positioned relative to a page: it is a child of a `<Spread>`, and its coordinates are
*spread* coordinates, where the spine is at x = 0. Each `<Page>` is placed in that same
space by its own `ItemTransform`. Inside every item there is a second space, its own,
in which its outline is drawn; the item's `ItemTransform` maps that inner space into its
parent's space. This chapter covers the spaces, the transforms between them, and how an
outline is written.

## Three spaces

Reading coordinates comes down to knowing which space a number lives in:

| Space | What lives there | Mapped into its parent by |
|---|---|---|
| **Item space** | an item's `PathGeometry` anchors and its `GeometricBounds` | the item's `ItemTransform` |
| **Parent space** | the spread, an enclosing `Group`, or the frame an item was pasted into | the parent's own `ItemTransform`, up to the spread |
| **Spread space** | every page item once its transforms are applied, and every page | the spread's `ItemTransform` (onto the pasteboard) |

A page has an inner space too: its `GeometricBounds` are written in it, and its
`ItemTransform` maps them into spread space. The two pages of a facing spread sit side by
side in spread space because their transforms put the left page at negative x and the
right page at positive x. [Units & origin](/docs/idml/geometry-coordinates/units-and-origin)
works through a real two-page spread.

A placed image adds one more level: the `<Image>` inside a frame carries its own
`ItemTransform`, relative to the frame. That is how an image is scaled and cropped inside
its frame. <SupportBadge feature="geometry-coordinates.image-content-transform" />

## Frequently asked questions

**What coordinate system does IDML use?**
Points, with X growing to the right and Y growing downward. Page items use the coordinate
space of their spread, whose origin is on the spine. Each item also has an inner space for
its own outline, mapped into its parent by its `ItemTransform`.

**Are page item coordinates relative to the page?**
No. Page items are children of the `<Spread>`, not of a `<Page>`, and their transforms map
into spread space. To find an item's position on a page, subtract the page's own placement
in the spread.

**Why does a shape's outline start at 0, 0 when the shape is in the middle of the page?**
Because the outline is written in the item's own space. The `ItemTransform` then moves that
space to where the item sits. Position and outline are stored separately.
