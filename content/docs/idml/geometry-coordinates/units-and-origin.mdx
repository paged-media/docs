**IDML coordinates are points, Y grows downward, and page items are placed in their spread's space, whose origin is on the spine.**

**In short:** Every coordinate in a layout part is a plain number in points (1/72 inch).
X grows to the right and Y grows downward. The origin that page items are measured from
is not a page corner: items belong to a spread, and spread coordinates put x = 0 on the
spine, the fold between the left and right page. InDesign's own files also put y = 0 at
the pages' vertical centre. Each `<Page>` is placed in spread space by its own
`ItemTransform`, so the left page of a facing spread lies at negative x.

## The unit is the point

Bounds, transforms and path anchors carry no unit. They are all points: 72 to the inch.
That is why metric documents are full of non-round numbers: an A4 page is
`595.276 × 841.89` (210 × 297 mm), US Letter is `612 × 792`.

## Y grows downward

X grows to the right, as usual. Y grows **downward**: a larger Y is further down the page.
A box from `y = 100` to `y = 300` is 200 pt tall, with its bottom edge at the larger value.
This holds in every space of the format: item space, page space and spread space.

## Page items use spread coordinates

A spread part holds one `<Spread>` element. Its children are the `<Page>` elements *and*
the page items: text frames, rectangles, groups and the rest are siblings of the pages,
not children of them. Their transforms map into the spread's space.

In that space:

- **x = 0 is the spine.** On a facing spread the left page runs from `-width` to `0` and
  the right page from `0` to `width`. A single-page spread puts its page to the right of
  the spine, from `0` to `width`.
- **y = 0 is the vertical centre** of the pages in the files InDesign writes: a page of
  height `H` runs from `-H/2` to `H/2`.
- **Each page is placed by its `ItemTransform`.** InDesign writes the left page of an A4
  spread with `ItemTransform="1 0 0 1 -595.276 -420.945"` and the right page with
  `1 0 0 1 0 -420.945`; both have `GeometricBounds="0 0 841.89 595.276"` in their own
  space.

The vertical centring is a convention, not a rule a reader may rely on. What a reader can
rely on is the arithmetic: a page's rectangle in spread space is its `GeometricBounds`
mapped through its `ItemTransform`. Some files put the page tops at `y = 0`, and some
InDesign exports carry page bounds that do not start at `0 0` (a page whose bounds start
at `24.94` and whose transform subtracts `420.94` still spans `-396 … 396`). Both are
placed correctly by doing the mapping.

## Which page an item is on

Nothing in an item names its page. A reader works it out from geometry: map the item's
bounds into spread space and find the page that contains it. Items that lie outside every
page sit on the **pasteboard**, the area around the pages; InDesign keeps them in the file
and does not print them.

> **In Paged:** an item belongs to the page that contains the centre of its bounds, and
> it is drawn on every page its bounds overlap, so an item across the spine shows on both
> pages.

## Spreads on the pasteboard

The `<Spread>` element has an `ItemTransform` too. InDesign uses it to stack the spreads
of a document down its pasteboard: the first spread at `0 0`, the next one a page height
plus a gap further down (`1 0 0 1 0 1039.89` for A4), and so on. Because the pages and the
items of a spread are both inside that transform, the translation does not change where an
item sits on its page. A rotation in it does: it turns the whole spread, as InDesign's
*rotate spread view* does.

## Item space

One level further in, each item draws its outline in its own space, usually from
`0, 0`. The item's `ItemTransform` maps that space into the spread (or into a group, or
into the frame the item was pasted into). The unit and the direction of Y are the same
in every space; only the origin and orientation change.
[`ItemTransform`](/docs/idml/geometry-coordinates/item-transform) covers the matrix.

InDesign writes an `ItemTransform` on every spread, page and page item, including the
identity `1 0 0 1 0 0`. A writer should do the same: InDesign does not read a missing
`ItemTransform` as the identity, and items written without one land in the wrong place.

## Frequently asked questions

**What unit does IDML use for coordinates?**
The point, 1/72 of an inch, everywhere. Nothing in the file labels a value as points.

**Where is the origin for page items?**
On the spine of the spread: page items are children of the `<Spread>` and use its
coordinates. In InDesign's own files the origin is also at the vertical centre of the
pages. The page's top-left corner is the origin only of the page's own inner space.

**Why does a frame on the left page have a negative x?**
Because the left page of a facing spread lies left of the spine, at negative x in spread
coordinates, and the frame's `ItemTransform` is written in those coordinates.

**How do I convert an item's position to page coordinates?**
Map the item's bounds through its transforms into spread space, then subtract the page's
placement: map the page's `GeometricBounds` through the page's `ItemTransform` and measure
from that rectangle's top-left corner.

**Why is 595.276 such a common number?**
It is 210 mm, the A4 width, in points (210 × 72 / 25.4). 841.89 is the A4 height.
