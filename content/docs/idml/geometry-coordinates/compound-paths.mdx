**A compound path is one IDML shape built from several contours: the way a hole is punched into a fill.**

**In short:** A [`PathGeometry`](/docs/idml/geometry-coordinates/path-geometry) can hold
more than one `GeometryPathType`. Together the contours form *one* shape, filled and
stroked as one: a ring, the counter of a letter, a panel with a window. Nothing marks a
contour as outer or inner. Whether an inner contour cuts a hole depends on its winding
direction under the nonzero rule. Each contour is a separate closed (or open) loop.

## Two contours, one shape

The outer contour defines the filled region; an inner contour wound the **opposite** way
cancels it where they overlap, leaving a hole. Under the nonzero winding rule, loops wound
the same way add up to a solid shape instead.

  An outer square and an inner square wound the opposite way: the overlap cancels into a hole.
</ExampleEmbed>

The outer contour runs `0,0 → 240,0 → 240,240 → 0,240`; the inner one runs the reverse
way. Reverse the inner contour's points so both wind the same way and the hole fills in.

## Each contour is its own loop

Every `GeometryPathType` starts a new loop. A reader must close each contour back to *its
own* first point, not run on from the end of one contour into the start of the next. A
consumer that merges all points into one list and draws it as a single polygon joins the
outer and inner squares with a stray segment, and the hole never forms.

Each contour also carries its own `PathOpen`. A compound path can therefore mix closed
contours with open ones; each closes, or stays open, on its own terms.

## Where compound paths come from

Compound paths come from InDesign's *Make Compound Path* command and from Pathfinder
operations such as subtract and exclude overlap. Any shape element can hold several
contours; a reader should not assume only `<Polygon>` does.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a compound path in IDML?**
A single `PathGeometry` with more than one `GeometryPathType`. The contours form one shape
with several loops, which is how holes are made.

**How does a compound path punch a hole?**
By winding. Under the nonzero rule, an inner contour wound opposite to the outer one cancels
the fill where they overlap. The file has no flag for "inner".

**Is each contour closed separately?**
Yes. Each `GeometryPathType` is its own loop with its own `PathOpen`. Joining the end of one
contour to the start of the next is a reader error that fills the hole in.
